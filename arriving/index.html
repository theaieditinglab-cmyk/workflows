01 · The idea

One ride, one night, six techniques.

Ride ads usually show a phone and a smiling passenger. Here the product is the feeling of the ride itself: rain on the window, real streets the app reads one by one, lights streaking past, a countdown and the headlights of the car that arrives. Black and white keeps it premium, and the city lights carry all the color.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. The word "Uber" is set in type, and the driver details on screen are made up.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

A night piece lives or dies on light. Every scene needs something that glows, or the phone screen just shows black.

A technique per scene

A rain shader, a real map turned into a LiDAR scan, long-exposure trails that write, a split-flap board and headlights through the rain. None of them repeat.

Brand rules

The word set in type, no logo files, invented driver details, and "Uber is a trademark of Uber Technologies, Inc." in the caption.

Palette

Near-black and white, warm amber street lights, red tail lights and a cool blue for the scan.

#030405#FFFFFF#FFB86B#FF2A1F#CFE0FF#06C167
Type

Inter Tight 900 for every headline, also "Any ride.", which is written by light trails; JetBrains Mono for the HUD. No handwritten fonts.

Pace

One scene and one line about every 3 seconds, the rain already falling on frame 0, every headline on screen for at least 1.5 seconds.

Format

9:16, 24 s, 30 fps, with every card and headline inside the Reels safe zone and never white text over a light area.

Sound

A slow synthwave made in code, rain that follows the glass scenes, one sound for every event and a seven-line narration.

Before the prompt.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: a 3-second test, stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 24-second vertical spec ad for Uber called "Night Ride" in Remotion (React) with @remotion/three, 1080x1920 at 30 fps. The idea: one ride at night, black and white with city light as the only color, and a different technique every 3 seconds. Render with --gl=angle. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

Look: near-black (#030405), white type, warm city lights and red tail lights as the only color. Type: Inter Tight 900 for every headline (no handwritten or decorative fonts) and JetBrains Mono for small labels. The word "Uber" is set in type, no logo files.

Scenes, in order:
1. 0-3s: rain on glass. Write a fragment shader: behind the glass, 90 blurred city lights (bokeh); on the glass, two layers of round drops that slide down in jerks plus small static droplets. Each drop is a lens: inside it, sample the lights sharper and shifted by the drop's normal, darken its rim and add a small highlight. On frame 0 the rain is already moving and a white search bar types "Where to?", then chips Home, Airport, Downtown, and a tap.
2. 3-6s: a real map. Download Midtown Manhattan from OpenStreetMap with the Overpass API (buildings with their height, streets with their lanes, crossings, traffic signals, trees) and turn it into a point cloud with a Python script, like a real LiDAR scan: building walls floor by floor, sparse asphalt, curbs, sidewalks, lane dashes and the double center line, zebra crosswalks, street lamps, traffic signals, trees and parked cars along the curb. Give each point an intensity (painted lines reflect the most). Seen from straight above it reads as the Manhattan grid; a white route draws itself along real streets in the real direction of traffic (down 7th Avenue from W 47th St, left on W 42nd St to 6th Avenue). Then the camera tilts down to street level while the buildings rise. Credit "Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors" on screen.
3. 6-9s: the camera flies down the real street at driver height, between real facades, crosswalks and parked cars, and scan rings pulse out from the car and light up the points. Headline "Every street, scanned."
4. 9-12s: a crossing at night seen from above: long-exposure light trails, red tail lights and warm white headlights, drawn as tubes that grow with time plus a wider additive halo. Two light streaks (a white headlight and a red tail light) rise from the crossing and write "Any ride." in Inter Tight 900: the outline draws on, then the fill glows like a long exposure.
5. 12-15s: a 3D split-flap board over the rain: "ARRIVING IN", then a big flap that falls from 3 to 2 to 1, and "MIN". Each flap is two halves that rotate in CSS 3D.
6. 15-19s: no modeled car (a car built from boxes looks like a cartoon). The ride arrives as light, in the same rain shader: two headlights far away on frame 0 that grow and separate as they come closer, focused and flipped inside every raindrop, with a long shimmering reflection on the wet road and a horizontal lens streak. They dim when the car stops, and a white card says "Your driver is here".
7. 19-24s: back to the rain on glass, "Uber" fades in from blur and "Wherever tonight takes you." End there.

Keep every headline and card inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. A small HUD at the bottom shows the ETA counting down during scenes 2 to 4.

Audio: an original slow synthwave in D-flat minor at 84 BPM made in code (numpy): warm detuned saw pad, an echoing arpeggio, a sub bass and a big reverb snare from the flight to the arrival. Rain that is loud on the glass scenes and quiet elsewhere. SFX on real events only: soft key taps, the tap, a rising tone as the route draws, sonar pings for each scan ring, cars passing with doppler at the crossing, a cascade of flap clacks, an electric motor and tires on wet road, an original two-note "driver is here" chime and a low hit on the logo. Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice af_heart: "Where to tonight?" / "Pick a place. We'll find the way." / "Every street, mapped in real time." / "Any ride, any hour." / "Your driver is close." / "And right on time." / "Uber. Wherever tonight takes you." Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: first render a 3-second test of the rain at half resolution. Then export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: a Remotion composition with one rain shader for the glass scenes, one three.js world for the real map, the street scan and the crossing, headlights inside the rain shader, a camera that changes per scene, the split-flap board and cards on top, and the music, rain, SFX and narration on the timeline.

00:02 · stills before render · claude code

Tell me if the drops read as lenses or as dark holes, if anything blocks the camera when it goes down to street level, if the point cloud is bright enough to read on a phone, and if the car stays fully in frame when it stops.

05 · What Claude built

A night you can render.

RAIN

Every drop is a lens

Drops are round in screen space, slide down in jerks and leave droplets behind. Inside each one the shader samples the city lights sharper and shifted, like a real lens.

CITY

Real streets as a LiDAR scan

858,000 points built from OpenStreetMap: facades floor by floor with their real height, lane lines, crosswalks, lamps, trees and parked cars. Flat as a map at first, they rise as the camera tilts, and a scan ring around the car lights them up.

MUSIC

Composed in code

A slow synthwave in D-flat minor with a pad, an echoing arpeggio and a big reverb snare, under rain that rises and falls with the glass scenes.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS

Seven short lines in the Heart voice. The music ducks under the voice and the mix sits at −14 LUFS with a true peak at −3 dB.

key code · glsl
// rain on glass: every drop is a lens
vec3 a = drops(uv, uT, 9.0, 0.0), b = drops(uv + vec2(0.21, 0.37), uT * 1.15, 13.0, 13.0);
vec2 nrm = a.xy + b.xy;                    // the drop's normal
float m = clamp(a.z + b.z, 0.0, 1.0);      // where there is water
vec3 outside = bokeh(uv, 1.0);             // blurred city lights
vec3 inside  = bokeh(uv - nrm * 0.11, 0.12) * 1.25;   // sharper, shifted
vec3 col = mix(outside, inside, m);
col *= 1.0 - 0.55 * smoothstep(0.55, 1.0, length(nrm)) * m;   // dark rim

// real streets as LiDAR: each point has an intensity (paint reflects the most)
p.y *= uRise;
float scan = exp(-abs(length(p.xz - uCar.xz) - ring * 60.0) * 0.9);
vB = (0.25 + 0.75 * aI) * uGain + scan * 2.2;

// the ride arrives as two headlights behind the wet glass
vec2 c = vec2(side * mix(0.012, 0.15, a * a), mix(-0.03, -0.14, a));
col += vec3(0.93, 0.96, 1.0) * (disc * 2.4 + glow * 0.9) * uHLon;

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

A new idea, not a repeat

The first plan was an infinite zoom, but we had already used it in another ad. A new world for every piece keeps the feed surprising.

02

Night, and light as the color

Black and white keeps it premium. The only color comes from things that glow: street lights, tail lights, the scan.

03

Open and close on the glass

The rain on the window opens and closes the spot, so it feels like one ride from the back seat.

04

The app's words, not ours

"Where to?", the ETA, "Your driver is here": the copy is what the viewer already sees in the app, so it reads instantly.

05

A countdown you can hear

The split-flap board turns waiting into a moment, and its clacks are the most satisfying sound in the spot.

06

Stills before render

Three rounds of stills caught every problem in minutes instead of hours of rendering.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Drops that look like holes

If a drop only darkens what is behind it, it reads as a hole. It has to show the lights sharper and shifted, with a dark rim and a small highlight, and there have to be many lights behind the glass.

Round drops

Drops are laid out in tall cells so they can slide. Measure each drop in screen units, not cell units, or they come out as long ovals.

A line that becomes a wall

A route drawn as a 3D tube looks great from above and becomes a white wall when the camera goes down to the street. Flatten it and hide it before the camera arrives.

Boxes look fake

A city or a car built from boxes reads as a cartoon. Use real data (a map, a licensed 3D model) or tell it with light: headlights, reflections, silhouettes.

Data that loads late

ThreeCanvas only redraws when the frame changes. If a point cloud arrives with fetch, call advance() once it loads and only then release the render.

Sharp sounds push the peak

Flap clacks are short and loud. If the true peak goes above −1.5 dB, limit a little harder before encoding.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Dark scenes with fine points band and block when compressed. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame with the most light. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 28M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "alimiter=limit=0.8:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
