01 · The idea

One late trip, one idea per block.

Everyone has been late with a red road ahead. That is the whole story: you are late, Maps finds the way around, and you arrive on time. Each block adds one real Maps feature (live traffic, rerouting, route options, sharing your trip, Live View, the place card) and each one gets its own view, so the eye always has something new while the story stays simple.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. There is no footage, screen recording or logo file: the map, the cards and the 4-colour pin are drawn in code.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

A product ad for an app only works if you recognise the app in one glance and understand the story on the first watch.

The app's own language

The thick blue route with its darker casing, the blue dot with a soft halo, the red pin, white rounded cards and a red line for traffic. That vocabulary carries the brand in every block, long before the name appears.

Real features only

Live traffic, rerouting, the route-options sheet, sharing trip progress, Live View walking arrows and the place card with hours and rating. One per block, shown the way the app shows it.

Palette

The four Maps colours on a cream paper map, with a dark-navy night map for the one moment Maps finds the new way.

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) for your system, and open it inside an empty project folder.

Step 02

A way to render a canvas to video.

Any setup that draws an HTML canvas as a pure function of time and captures it frame by frame works, for example Remotion (npx create-video@latest) or a headless browser piping frames into FFmpeg. Install Node 22+ and FFmpeg.

Step 03

An ElevenLabs API key.

Create a key in your ElevenLabs account and keep it in an environment variable, never in the code. Ask the API for the audio with timestamps, so every word comes back with its start and end time.

Step 04

Python for the checks.

Python with Pillow builds the contact sheets of stills; FFmpeg measures loudness and does the final encode.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 30-second vertical spec ad for Google Maps called "Every road, already figured out." in 1080x1920 at 30 fps, drawn entirely in code on an HTML canvas. No photos, no screen recordings and no logo files: draw the Maps UI yourself (the blue route with a darker casing, the blue you-dot with its halo, the red destination pin, white rounded cards) and draw the 4-colour pin in code. Every value must be a pure function of the time t.

The story is one trip, told in one simple line per block: you're late → the road ahead turns red → Maps reroutes you → you share your arrival → Live View arrows walk you the last block → the place card → you arrive at 10:00 → brand. One idea per block, and every block gets its own view and one real Maps feature.

Narration (one voice, natural speed):
"You're already late, and the road ahead just turned red. Google Maps sees it before you do, and bends your blue line two streets over. Six minutes faster. One tap shares your arrival, so the people waiting watch you get closer, live. Park, and the last block shows up right on the street, with arrows you can't miss. The café is open, four point six stars, and your table is ready. You walk in at ten, on the dot. Google Maps. Every road, already figured out."

Generate it with the ElevenLabs API (voice "Jessica", natural speed) and ask for word-level timestamps. Save every word with its start and end time, and use those times as animation cues: every pop, flip and roll lands on a spoken word.

Blocks and views:
1. 0-3.6s, drop: frame 0 already moving. We come down through soft paper clouds while the red pin falls onto a cream paper city map. A route card reads "10:00 · meeting"; on "late" the ETA rolls to 10:06 in red; on "red" the stretch ahead turns red and the dot stops.
2. 3.6-8s, reroute: close on the jam. A "Heavy traffic ahead +6 min" alert card on "sees". On "bends" the map switches to a dark-navy night map from the dot outward, and the blue line redraws two streets over with a glowing head; markers 1 and 2 pop on the two cross streets.
3. 8-9.1s, faster: a Maps route-options sheet rises. "via 5th St" gets a green "6 min faster" chip and its ETA rolls 10:06 → 10:00; the old route is struck through.
4. 9.1-14.5s, share: back to paper, inside a drawn phone. A finger taps "Share trip progress"; a chat card from Sofia shows "Arriving 10:00" with a mini live map; "LIVE" pulses on "live".
5. 14.5-19.1s, Live View: a calm street-level paper street in one-point perspective ending at Café Aurora. "Parked" chip on "Park", big blue Live View arrows rise on the sidewalk on "street" and march forward on "arrows", the destination pin floats over the door on "miss".
6. 19.1-23.4s, place card: "Café Aurora", "Open now" on "open", 4.6 with stars filling on "stars", "Table ready" on "ready".
7. 23.4-25.3s, on time: close on the last block, the dot reaches the pin on "ten", a clock clicks to 10:00 and the card turns green "Arrived · on time".
8. 25.3-30s, brand: the map folds into a big 4-colour pin, "Google Maps" and the slogan type under it, then a small "Made with Claude · The AI Editing Lab" card. Keep the right corner (x 560-1080, y 1220-1920) empty: a presenter clip goes there later.

The times on every card must add up: meeting at 10:00, ETA 10:06 (6 min late), 6 min faster, arrive at 10:00.

Look: Google Maps colours (#4285F4 blue, #34A853 green, #FBBC04 yellow, #EA4335 red) on cream paper #F6F0E6, with navy #141A2E only for the night-map reroute. Calm camera: one slow push per block, no whips, no shakes. Captions on top, one or two words at a time. Keep every headline and card inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480.

Sound: one soft effect per event, each on its cue word (a tick on "late", a thud on "red", a swoosh for the alert, a click on the tap, a ding on arrival). Duck them under the voice and master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: export one still every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked, and check that frame 0 already moves and every block looks different from the one before. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: the voice with a word list and timestamps, one scene file per block on a shared map world (the paper city, the routes, the you-dot, the pin and the route card, all functions of time), and the sound cues pinned to their words.

00:02 · stills before render · claude code
Before rendering, export one still every 1.5 seconds, starting at frame 0, with a red outline marking the Instagram Reels safe zone (left 90, right 960, top 250, bottom 1480 px), all in one contact sheet. Then check: does every block open on a new view, or do two neighbouring blocks look the same? Is the route card readable at phone size? Do the times add up across every card (10:00, 10:06, 6 min faster, 10:00)? Does each pop land on its word? Is the right corner empty for the presenter in the last 5 seconds? Also run a motion check (does every block move from its first frame?) and compare each block against the others so no two repeat the same layout.

What came back: a contact sheet of the whole spot, a motion check for every block and a comparison between blocks. Anything that read too small at phone size, a pop that landed between words or a card whose time did not match the story was fixed on stills, before the render.

00:03 · final render · claude code
Approved. Render the final at 1080x1920 and 30 fps and encode it at 14 Mbps CBR with the mix. Then composite the presenter clip in the right corner from 25.3 s to the end, and slide it in time so her wink lands exactly on the last spoken word ("out"). Check the loudness and give me the video with and without the presenter.

What came back: 30 seconds at 14 Mbps, with the presenter's wink landing on the last word.

05 · What Claude built

One map world, many views.

MAP

A paper city that never cuts

Blocks, streets, a park and a grid of 7 by 7 roads. The route, the jam, the dot and the pin all live on it, so later blocks can come back to the same trip.

NIGHT MAP

A palette switch, not a cut

On "bends" the map floods to dark navy from the dot outward. It is the one moment the colours change, so the reroute feels like Maps taking over.

PHONE

The same trip, smaller

The map shrinks into a drawn phone and a chat card with a mini live map. Your dot keeps moving on both.

STREET

Live View in one-point perspective

A paper street projected from a simple camera: shopfronts, lamps, two walkers and the café at the end, with blue chevrons lying on the sidewalk.

the blue route redraw · javascript
// the reroute: the new blue line draws itself two streets over on "blue"
const GM_N = [[540, 1250], [540, 1220], [820, 1220], [820, 660], [540, 660]];   // the new route
const bendP = E.inOutCubic(clamp((t - GT.blue) / 1.0));                        // 0 → 1 in one second
if (bendP > 0) gmRoute(K, gmSub(GM_N, from, GM_LN * bendP), t, { lw: 24, speed: 90 });

// the Maps route line: dark casing + blue core + flowing light dashes
function gmRoute(K, pts, t, o = {}) {
  const a = o.a ?? 1, lw = o.lw ?? 22;
  gmStroke(K, pts, o.case || GM_BLUE_D, lw + 10, { a });
  gmStroke(K, pts, o.col || GM_BLUE, lw, { a });
  gmStroke(K, pts, 'rgba(255,255,255,0.55)', 5, { a, dash: [3, 30], off: -t * 70 });
}
word cues + the ETA roll · javascript
// cue words come straight from the voice's word timestamps
const GT = { late: wi(2), red: wi(9), sees: wi(12), bends: wi(18), blue: wi(20),
             six: wi(25), faster: wi(27), tap: wi(29), park: wi(42), ten: wi(75), out: wiE(85) };

// minutes past 10:00 on the card: 0 → 6 on "late", rolls back 6 → 0 from "six" to "faster"
const gmCardMin = t => {
  if (t < GT.late) return 0;
  if (t < GT.six) return 6 * E.outCubic(clamp((t - GT.late) / 0.5));
  return lerp(6, 0, E.inOutCubic(clamp((t - GT.six) / (GT.faster + 0.35 - GT.six))));
};
odometer(K, gmCardMin(t), x, y, { size: 76, prefix: '10:0', digits: 1 });   // the ETA digit rolls
live view arrows · javascript
// Live View: chevrons projected onto the sidewalk, rising one by one on "street", marching on "arrows"
function lvP(S, x, y, z) { const d = Math.max(0.35, z - S.cz); return [540 + x * F_ / d, S.hor + (CAMH - y) * F_ / d]; }
function lvArrows(K, S, t) {
  if (t < GT.street - 0.02) return;
  const march = t > GT.arrows ? ((t - GT.arrows) * 1.3) % 1.7 : 0;
  for (let i = 5; i >= 0; i--) {                                   // far → near
    const z = S.cz + 2.2 + i * 1.7 + march;
    const rise = clamp(pop(t - GT.street - i * 0.09, 2.6, 0.5), 0, 1.2);
    const fadeIn = clamp((z - S.cz - 2.0) / 0.6), fadeOut = clamp((ZE - 1.0 - z) / 1.2);
    if (z > ZE - 0.8) continue;
    lvChevron(K, S, z, Math.min(1, rise) * fadeIn * fadeOut, (1 - Math.min(1, rise)) * -0.5);
  }
}

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

One simple story

An earlier 3D version tried to explain the whole traffic mechanism and moved too fast to follow. One trip, one idea per block, made it clear on the first watch.

02

A new view every block

The next version was clear but stayed on the same map for 20 seconds and felt flat. At 30 seconds, every block got its own view and its own real Maps feature.

03

Draw the brand, don't paste it

No logo files: the 4-colour pin and the whole Maps UI are drawn in code, and the app's look carries the brand from the first frame.

04

Times that add up

10:00 meeting, 10:06 late, 6 min faster, 10:00 arrival. One function feeds every card, so the numbers can never disagree.

05

Words as cues

The voice came back with word timestamps, so each pop, roll and flip is pinned to the word that names it.

06

A calm camera

One slow push per block and a gentle reframe on the stressed word. No whips, no shakes, nothing that competes with the story.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Too much mechanism

Explaining how traffic data works (cars, sensors, layers) made an earlier version hard to follow. Show what the user sees in the app, one feature at a time.

The same frame for 20 seconds

A clear story on one view still feels flat. If two neighbouring blocks look alike in the contact sheet, give one of them a new view.

Numbers that don't add up

If the late time, the minutes saved and the arrival time come from separate hard-coded strings, they drift apart. Compute them all from one function of time.

A wink after the voice

A presenter reacting after the last word feels late. Slide the clip until the gesture lands exactly on the last spoken word.

Leave room for the presenter

Keep the corner where the presenter goes empty in the last block, or her clip covers the slogan.

Small UI text

App cards are dense. Check every card at phone size on the stills: if a line needs a squint, make it bigger or cut it.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Thin map streets and small card text smear at low bitrates. Encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

A frame where the Maps world reads at once, with the brand name and the slogan. The feed crops covers to 4:5 and the profile grid to 3:4, so keep everything between y 300 and 1480.

Step 04

A short caption with the credits.

What the piece is, that it was made with Claude Code, the keyword, and the credits: "Spec ad · not affiliated with or endorsed by Google" and "Voice: ElevenLabs". End with "Inspired by @googlemaps".
