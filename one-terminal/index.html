01 · The idea

Twelve minutes on the clock.

The film is a small story with a deadline. A message arrives at 4:47: a launch film, with music, voice, 3D and streets, by 5. One prompt in a terminal opens six agents, and the camera goes inside to watch them work in parallel. A countdown stays on screen the whole time, so every block reads as minutes being spent.

Each station shows something real instead of a stand-in: a real 3D car model, a real street map, a QA panel with the actual checks we run. Everything ends in one spark at the centre of the studio, and then in the finished film.

02 · What Claude needed

What went in before the first prompt.

A 3D model with a licence

"Porsche 911 with interior" by n.brizitskaya (CC BY 4.0), a lighter copy from a public GitHub mirror with its licence file. It was already in the project from an earlier film, with its loader (paint, wheels, cleaned-up badges).

Real map data

Midtown Manhattan from OpenStreetMap, downloaded with Overpass and turned by a Python script into a point cloud like a LiDAR scan: facades floor by floor at their real heights, asphalt, lane lines, crossings. 857,709 points in one binary file.

The Lab's own brand

No client here. Cream, ink and clay from the Lab's palette, the Claude spark, and Bricolage Grotesque, Geist and Geist Mono for the type. Each agent gets its own neon colour so the six stations read at a glance.

Our rules for reels

Text and product inside the Instagram safe zone (x 90–960, y 250–1480), no music unless asked for, one idea per block, and a stills check before any final render.

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) and open it in your project folder.

Step 02

Remotion with three.js.

npx create-video@latest, then npm i @remotion/three three @react-three/fiber @react-three/drei @react-three/postprocessing. Render WebGL with --gl=angle.

Step 03

Python for data and sound.

Python with NumPy builds the map point cloud and writes every sound effect. The street data comes from OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API.

Step 04

A free, offline voice.

Kokoro (the kokoro-onnx package) runs on your own machine. FFmpeg mixes the voice with the effects, measures loudness and does the final encode.

04 · The brief

What we asked for, and the passes.

The original prompts of this session were not saved word for word. The block below is the brief, reconstructed from the caption, our production sheet and what the final code does. The timings and texts in it are the ones in the film.

brief · reconstructed from the caption and the plan
Make a 28-second vertical film for The AI Editing Lab, 1080x1920 at 30 fps, in Remotion with three.js. All in English, no brand: it is our own piece. The idea: one terminal is a whole studio.

Story, on a clock that never stops (4:47 PM to 4:59 PM, the delivery is at 5:00):
1. 0-3.5 s: a message from the boss lands: "Need a launch film." / "Music. Voice. 3D. Streets." / "By 5." A giant 4:47 sits behind it and ticks to 4:48.
2. 3.5-7 s: the giant clock flies up into a countdown bar that stays on screen (time, progress, minutes left; red in the last two and a half minutes). A Claude Code terminal rises, types "make the launch film. all of it." and opens six agents, each one running.
3. 7-12 s: the camera pushes through the terminal into a neon 3D studio seen from above: six glowing stations, one per agent (research, 3D model, real map, music, voice, QA), with the Claude spark at the centre sending pulses to each one. Title: "Six agents. One terminal."
4. 12-20 s: three close-ups, each with its agent chip: a real Porsche 911 model turning on its pad ("A real 3D car. Built in code."), Midtown Manhattan from OpenStreetMap as a glowing point cloud ("Real streets. Real map data."), and a QA panel ticking safe zone, text size, no text on faces, -14 LUFS ("It checks its own work.").
5. 20-22 s: back up to the whole studio, the pulses speed up into the spark ("All at once.") and a warm flash.
6. 22-28 s: the finished film: the Porsche driving into streets of light. "4:59", "Approved", "One terminal. A whole studio." and The AI Editing Lab with the spark.

Use real assets: the CC BY Porsche model (credit the author), real OpenStreetMap data (credit OpenStreetMap contributors). No music. A voice line per block, free and offline (Kokoro), with 2-3 voice samples to choose from. Sound effects written in code, one per event on screen, in the feel of a calm control room. Mix to -14 LUFS, peak -1.5 dB.

Keep every text and the product inside x 90-960, y 250-1480. Before the final render, give me a still every 1.5 s with the safe zone marked.

What came back first: a different film. Version 1 was a sea of light particles. It looked fine and said nothing about what Claude can do.

pass 1 · the feedback, in our words
V1 (a sea of light particles) was rejected: too basic, it doesn't show what the tool can actually do. New concept: show the real capabilities. Agents working in parallel, a real 3D model, real map data, a QA step that checks the work.

What came back: version 2, the studio: three key frames first (the terminal, the six stations from above, the final shot), then the full film with the countdown. Approved.

pass 2 · the feedback, in our words
Two fixes, nothing else: the spark has to sit in the centre of the six stations (from the top camera it shows between 01 and 02; only the cover is right), and the whooshes are too loud and harsh. Make them lower and softer, and the clock ticks less sharp.

What came back: version 3, the one that was posted. The spark moved down to the centre of the stations, and the whooshes were capped at 1,800 Hz and brought down to 40 % of their level.

const storySec = (t: number) => {
  if (t <= 0) return CLOCK[0][1];
  for (let i = 0; i < CLOCK.length - 1; i++) {const [a, sa] = CLOCK[i], [b, sb] = CLOCK[i + 1]; if (t <= b) return sa + (sb - sa) * (t - a) / (b - a);}
  return CLOCK[CLOCK.length - 1][1];
};

pulses from the spark to each station · Studio.tsx
const Beams: React.FC<{t: number; boost: number; o: number}> = ({t, boost, o: bo}) => (
  <group>{AG.map((a, i) => {
    const s = new THREE.Vector3(...CORE), e = new THREE.Vector3(a.pos[0], 0.9, a.pos[2]);
    const mid = s.clone().lerp(e, 0.5).add(new THREE.Vector3(0, 0.9, 0));
    const curve = new THREE.QuadraticBezierCurve3(s, mid, e);
    const geo = new THREE.TubeGeometry(curve, 48, 0.012, 6, false);
    const col = new THREE.Color(a.col).multiplyScalar(2.5);
    return <group key={i}>
      <mesh geometry={geo}><meshBasicMaterial color={col.clone().multiplyScalar(1 + 2.5 * boost)} toneMapped={false} transparent opacity={(0.55 + 0.45 * boost) * bo} /></mesh>
      {[0, 0.33, 0.66].map((o) => {const u = ((t * (0.55 + 1.6 * boost) + o + i * 0.13) % 1); const p = curve.getPoint(boost > 0.5 ? 1 - u : u); return <mesh key={o} position={p} visible={bo > 0.02}><sphereGeometry args={[0.045, 12, 12]} /><meshBasicMaterial color={new THREE.Color(a.col).multiplyScalar(6)} toneMapped={false} transparent opacity={bo} /></mesh>;})}
    </group>;
  })}</group>
);

07 · The sound

A calm control room, no music.

Voice

Kokoro, voice Nova (af_nova), free and offline. Three voices were sampled over the preview (George, Onyx, Nova) and Nova was chosen. Each line has a start time and a deadline; if a line runs long, it is regenerated slightly faster (up to 1.15×).

Effects, written in code

No sample library. Glass tones for the message, soft keys while the prompt types, one tuned FM blip per agent, a server hum under the studio, an idling engine for the car, data sparks for the map scan, two rising notes when QA passes, a low boom on the flash and a warm chord on "Approved". Every sound sits on an event in the film.

The clock

A tick each time the story minute changes, getting higher toward the end, and faster ticks in the last two minutes.

The mix

The effects duck under the voice (sidechain compression), then one fixed gain brings the mix to −14 LUFS with a −1.5 dB ceiling. The posted file measures −14.2 LUFS.

08 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

Show real capability

The particle version was pretty and empty. Agents in parallel, a real model, real data and a QA step are what the film is about.

02

A clock carries the story

The countdown never leaves the screen, so a tour of six stations reads as a race against 5 PM.

03

The spark in the middle

The spark sits at the centre of the six stations and every beam starts there. It has to look centred from the top camera, not only on the cover.

04

Soft, not loud

Harsh whooshes made the studio feel cheap. Lower, quieter air and softer ticks made it feel like a control room.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

Step 01

Check stills first.

One still every 1.5 s with the safe zone in red, before rendering 840 frames of WebGL.

Step 02

Render with ANGLE.

The composition is LabA-Film, 840 frames at 30 fps, rendered with --gl=angle so three.js works in the headless browser.

Step 03

Export at a real bitrate.

Thin neon lines and point clouds smear when compressed. The posted file is H.264 at about 14.8 Mbps with AAC audio.

Credit in the caption.
