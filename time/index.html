01 · The idea

Two years, two worlds.

The message fits in one sentence: in 2026 a 30-second ad took keyframes, a motion team and about three weeks; in 2027 it takes one prompt. The piece turns that into two physical worlds. The old way lives on a paper desk, where every cost arrives as a sticker and pushes the last one away. The new way lives in a one-color clay world, and the change between them is a pixel dissolve.

Then it proves the claim instead of just saying it: two finished reels play on screen, the old year gets struck through, and the piece signs off with "Claude cooked."

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Make a 30-second vertical editorial explainer in Remotion (React), 1080x1920 at 30 fps, 900 frames. Text and music only: no voice. Everything on screen in English.

Message: "In 2026 a 30-second ad took keyframes, a whole motion team and about 3 weeks. In 2027 it takes one prompt."

Brand. Use ONLY these colors: ink #141210, cream #F6F0E6, white paper #FFFFFF, clay #D97757 and deep clay #B9573A. Fonts from @remotion/google-fonts: Bricolage Grotesque 800 for the big words, Geist 400 for the small connector words, Geist Mono for labels.

Timing grid. The music is 120 BPM, so one beat = 15 frames. Every scene starts on a beat: hook 0, team 150, calendar 240, dissolve 330, prompt 345, proof 435, payoff 600, end card 780.

Two worlds.
- Act 1 (2026) is a paper desk seen from above: cream background, a 54 px grid of ink lines at 7% opacity, a warm deep-clay vignette at the edges and animated film grain (SVG fractal noise, multiply, 13%, new seed every 2 frames).
- Act 2 (2027) is a one-color clay world: a radial gradient from cream in the center to clay and deep clay at the edges, a 14 px dot pattern and 16% grain.
- Act 1 pieces are stickers: a white 14 px border with 26 px corners and a soft drop shadow. Each one springs in from the bottom right (spring damping 13, stiffness 120) and gets pushed out to the left (-950 px, rotating -10 degrees) when the next one arrives. No jitter, no shaking.

Type. Each line is a small connector (Geist 46 px) above ONE BIG WORD in uppercase (Bricolage 800, 132 px, line-height 0.95), centered at y 268. Words enter one by one, 5 frames apart: 9 frames with ease bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1), rising 26 px, scale 0.94 to 1, blur 8 to 0. Then a 10 px rounded underline in clay swipes under the full width of the big word. Measure the big word on a canvas and shrink the font if it is wider than 860 px. The line leaves as one block in 6 frames with a little blur. Every line stays up at least 1.5 seconds.

Scenes.
1. 0-2s, hook: on frame 0 a giant "2026" sticker (cream text on an ink plate, white border) is already on screen at 3.5x scale with 7 px of blur. In 1 second it drops onto the desk to 1x, slightly tilted, and its shadow appears. At frame 56 it gets pushed out to the left.
2. 2-5s: "In 2026, a 30-second ad / TOOK KEYFRAMES." + a dark timeline sticker: five clay bars with cream diamond keyframes, timecodes on top, a cream playhead sweeping, and a paper-tape label "keyframes x 400".
3. 5-8s: "a whole / MOTION TEAM" + three role cards that pop in 5 frames apart: DS Designer, AN Animator (clay circle), ED Editor.
4. 8-11s: "and about / 3 WEEKS." + a 21-day calendar sticker with an ink "PRODUCTION" header and a tape label "21 days". A clay X gets drawn over each day, one every 2.6 frames, each X as two strokes.
5. 10.9-11.5s: pixel dissolve into the clay world, bottom to top, in 54 px cells. Each cell switches when progress passes a threshold of (row position x 0.55 + random x 0.45), so it climbs but never looks like a straight wipe. 18 frames. Keep act 1 mounted until the dissolve ends.
6. 11.5-14.5s: "In 2027, it takes / ONE PROMPT." + a white prompt card with a spinning Claude spark, typing a real prompt with a blinking cursor, a clay send button, a gentle float, and a dotted ink orbit ring rotating around it.
7. 14.5-20s: "and Claude / COOKS THIS." + two of my finished reels playing (muted) as white-bordered cards, sliding in from the left and the right, tilted -5 and +5 degrees and floating.
8. 20-26s, payoff: "2026" fades in at 210 px in semi-transparent ink. At 21.1s an ink line strikes through it. At 21.7s "2027" springs in at 300 px, on the music's impact. At 23.3s the spark + "Claude cooked." (92 px, "cooked." in white paper) rises in.
9. 26-30s, end card: my logo + "Want the full workflow?" + an ink pill "Comment TIME" + "and we'll send you the prompt + workflow."

Safe zone: keep every text, sticker, card and the logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. Add a guides prop that draws this rectangle in red.

Audio. Compose an original score in code with numpy and scipy, 120 BPM, in A minor: a marimba ostinato over a sub bass. Act 1 is sparse (the marimba on half the steps, a ticking hat like a clock from 2s). A noise swell into an impact at 11.5s, the start of act 2, then the full groove: 2-step drums, sub line, short chord stabs. A second swell and impact when "2027" lands, and on the end card an impact, a rising marimba arpeggio and a long sub note. SFX few and soft, each on a real event: in act 1 the paper world (an air drop for the giant sticker, a paper slap when it lands, paper slides and drops for each sticker, card flicks for the team, a marker stroke per crossed-out day), a rising pixel sound on the dissolve, then the digital world (a tap when the prompt card appears, soft keys while it types, a send, air for the two reels), a marker on the strike-through, a thunk for 2027 and only ONE soft ting, on "Claude cooked." Write the music, SFX and mix as separate WAVs that add up, light reverb, fade the last 1.5s, master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process. First a 10-second test of the hook and the first two lines at half resolution. Before the full render, export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the red safe zone. Wait for my approval, apply my notes, and only then render the final MP4, plus a version without sound.

What came back: one 900-frame Remotion composition with a scene table on the beat grid, the paper desk and the clay world as background components, a reusable line component (connector + big word + underline), the sticker pieces, the pixel dissolve, a guides prop for the red safe zone and a sound prop for the version without audio. Plus one Python script that writes the music, the SFX and the mix.

05 · What Claude built

Two worlds, one beat grid.

STRUCTURE

A scene table in frames

Hook 0, team 150, calendar 240, dissolve 330, prompt 345, proof 435, payoff 600, end 780. All multiples of 15, so every scene starts on a beat.

TYPE

A line that fits itself

The big word is measured on a canvas and shrunk only if it passes 860 px. Words enter 5 frames apart and the clay underline swipes in after them.

PAPER

Stickers that push

Each sticker springs in from the bottom right and shoves the previous one out to the left, so act 1 reads as work piling up on a desk.

MUSIC

Composed in code

Marimba in A minor, a sub bass and a 2-step groove, with the impacts placed on the dissolve and on the frame where "2027" lands. Music, SFX and mix as stems.

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

The old way is physical

Paper, tape and stickers make 2026 feel manual and slow. The clay world after the dissolve feels clean and digital. The contrast is the argument.

02

One big word per screen

KEYFRAMES, MOTION TEAM, 3 WEEKS, ONE PROMPT. A small connector carries the sentence, the big word carries the idea, and it reads on a phone.

03

Prove it, don't claim it

"Cooks this" plays two real finished reels instead of a promise. The proof is the Lab's own work.

04

The sound changes worlds too

Paper slides, drops and marker strokes in act 1; taps, keys and a send in act 2. The groove only arrives with the dissolve.

05

Land the payoff on the music

"2027" springs in on the exact frame of the second impact. The strike-through comes first, so the eye is ready.

06

Brand colors only

Ink, cream, white and clay. No color from the reels it shows leaks into the design around them.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Frame 0 must already move

In another of our pieces, an opening with a small logo on an empty background read as a logo intro and did not hook. Here the "2026" sticker starts at 3.5x scale with blur on frame 0 and is still dropping when the viewer arrives.

Fast is not rushed

One of our tests packed five ideas into five seconds and was unreadable on a phone. Keep one idea per block of about three seconds and never less than 1.5 seconds for a line of text.

A dissolve, not a wipe

Pixels that switch row by row look like a cheap wipe. Mix the row position with a random value per cell (55/45) so it climbs from the bottom but breaks up. And no sparks across the whole frame: they look cheap.

Stay on the grid

At 120 BPM one beat is 15 frames. Put every scene start on a multiple of 15 and put the music's impacts on the frames that matter, or the cuts feel late.

The end card leaves the zone

Our logo wordmark at 92 px pushed its small diamond past x 960. In newer pieces the wordmark is set at 78 px. Check the last frame on the stills too.

Fewer, softer SFX

The tings were loved, but early mixes had them too loud and too many. One soft ting, on "Claude cooked.", and every other sound tied to something you see.

No paper jitter

A stop-motion shake on paper pieces was rejected in another piece. The stickers here move with smooth springs and never tremble.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Renderers often come out at 5–6 Mbps on flat designs, and Instagram compresses again. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the product best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the headline and the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the credits.

Say in one line how it was made and credit anything on screen that isn't yours. If you remake a real brand, end with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
