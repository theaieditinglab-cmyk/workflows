01 · The idea

One photo, everything else in code.

Our first take built the can in Blender. It looked like a render, not like the can in your hand, and a layered "how it's made" scene in the middle was hard to read. So this version starts from a real product photo, and Claude does the rest: it splits the can into layers, makes the flat photo turn like a cylinder, and builds every effect around it. Frost and drops drawn in code still looked fake, so the iced can is the same photo edited in Gemini (free), and the cold smoke is real footage.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. Monster publishes no brand guide, and the caption simply credits the brand: "Inspired by @monsterenergy".

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

The most-watched unofficial Monster ads are a real-looking can in ice and lightning cut to phonk. Meta's creative research points the same way for selling: show the product and the brand in the first seconds, show it more than once, and give it context.

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) for your system.

Step 02

Install Node 22+ and FFmpeg.

Start a Remotion project with npx create-video@latest. FFmpeg joins the render with the mix.

Step 03

Bring one clean photo.

The product from the front, sharp, on a plain background. Bigger is better: around 1500 px tall is enough for a full-height can. For the iced version, edit that same photo in the free Gemini app (keep the label and the logo, add frost and condensation) and save it on a white background.

Step 04

Python for the layers and the sound.

OpenCV and Pillow cut the layers and align the iced edit to the dry can, numpy and scipy compose the music and SFX. The cold smoke is free stock footage of smoke on black (Mixkit license). Kokoro TTS reads the narration and faster-whisper checks it.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Here is one product photo of a Monster Energy can on a white background, plus an iced version of the same photo I edited in Gemini, and three clips of real smoke on black. Build a 24-second vertical spec ad called "Unleash" in Remotion, 1080x1920 at 30 fps, 8 blocks of 3 seconds. Everything that moves is code.

Step 1, the can: cut the photo out of its white background and make four layers with Python and OpenCV: the clean can, the claw alone (green pixels above the wordmark), the can without the claw (inpaint over the black). Then align the iced edit to the dry can: AI edits drift (it came back about 9% wider, with the logo lower and a flat, wide lid), so measure reference rows in both (the lid rim, where the metal ends, the TAURINE band, the top and bottom of the claw, the wordmark, the base), warp the iced one piecewise to match, fit every row of the lid and neck to the exact width of the dry can, and use the dry silhouette for both. To make the flat photo turn, treat the can as a cylinder: each screen column sees the label at angle asin(x / r); to rotate by phi, sample the photo at asin(x / r) + phi, and correct the shading so the light stays put while the label turns. Keep it within about 35 degrees each way, the photo only shows the front.

Step 2, the motion: 0-3 s "UNLEASH THE BEAST." the can turns into view in green lightning, with a flash three frames in · 3-6 s "ICE COLD." an ice front sweeps up the can from the bottom (a hard mask with a bright band on the edge, never a crossfade, or you see two cans) and leaves the iced can, with cold vapor at its base · 6-9 s the iced can while a "473 ML · 16 fl oz · one can" counter climbs · 9-12 s a giant "160 MG" counter with lightning and two ingredient tags (taurine, Panax ginseng) · 12-15 s "MARKED BY THE CLAW." the claw is drawn in light from top to bottom over the can without it · 15-18 s "CRACK IT." the claw glows, and on the crack eight bolts shoot out from BEHIND the can to the edges with a shockwave · 18-21 s "SWEET. SALTY." on dark plates, the iced can turning slowly while real cold smoke puffs from the lid and rolls along the floor (screen blend, low opacity so it never veils the frame) · 21-24 s "GRAB ONE COLD." the iced can turns back to face you, then the logo lockup. When the can turns, keep the lid still: only the label rotates. Headlines in Barlow Condensed Black, uppercase, slightly skewed. Soft camera moves only (scale at or below 1.1).

Facts on screen come from monsterenergy.com (160 mg of caffeine per 16 fl oz, "sweet and salty") and the product label. Keep every headline, the can and the logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480, including at the peak of each push-in.

Audio: an original phonk track made in code (numpy), C-sharp minor at 120 BPM. And salty." / "Grab one cold. Unleash the beast." Check every line with Whisper. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

05 · What Claude built

A flat photo that turns.

SPIN

A cylinder from one photo

Each column of the can is read from the angle it would show on a real cylinder, so the claw and the letters bend toward the edge as it turns, while the highlights stay put.

LAYERS

Layers from one image

The can, the claw alone, the can without the claw (inpainted), and the iced edit warped onto the exact silhouette of the dry can, lid and neck included.

MUSIC

Phonk, composed in code

C-sharp minor at 120 BPM: a distorted 808 that slides between roots, a cowbell melody with echo, trap hats with rolls.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS, checked by Whisper

Eight short lines in the Fenrir voice. Whisper heard "Sweden salty" once, so the line became "Sweet. And salty." Mix at −14 LUFS, true peak −2 dB.

key code · the cylinder
# turn a flat photo: each column of the can sees the label at asin(x / r)
th = asin((x - cx) / r) + phi          # phi = how far the can has turned
sx = cx + r * sin(clamp(th, -84deg, 84deg))
# the light stays put, only the label turns
g  = shade(x) / shade(th)              # shade = 0.35 + 0.65 * cos
pixel = photo[y, sx] * g

A real photo looks like the can in your hand; code gives it motion, light and data. When code can't make something look real (ice, smoke), use a real image or real footage and let code place it. Effects that would cover the product go behind it.

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

The real can

The 3D can looked like a render. The real photo looks like the product people buy.

02

Keep the motion

A photo can't spin all the way around, but it can turn about 35 degrees each way. That was enough to bring back the movement of the first take.

03

One idea you can read

The layered "5 layers deep" scene was replaced by the can freezing and a 473 ml counter: one idea, understood at a glance.

04

Effects behind the product

Bolts that cross the can look like scratches on it. Behind the can, they look like they come out of it.

05

A voice with punch

Five voices tested on the same script; the deeper one fit the brand, at normal speed.

06

Stills before render

A fresh reviewer caught the fake-looking frost, the scratch-like drops and the covered lid before the final render.

07

A real iced can

Frost and drops drawn in code looked fake. One edit of the same photo in Gemini looks like a can out of the fridge.

08

Smoke, not a splash

Three versions of a liquid burst were rejected. Real cold smoke around the iced can sells "ice cold" without forcing it.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

How far it turns

A front photo only holds the front. Past about 35 degrees the edge shows nothing, so keep the turn small and land it facing front.

An AI edit changes the shape

The iced edit came back wider, with the logo lower and a flat lid, so the can "flattened" when it froze. Measure the same rows in both images, warp the edit onto the original, and keep the original silhouette.

Liquid that looks fake

A splash or fizz drawn in code, or pasted from another image, never sat on the can. If an effect doesn't convince after two tries, change the idea: real smoke footage did.

Push-ins and the safe zone

A can that fits at scale 1.0 can cross y 1480 at scale 1.07. Check the zone at the peak of every move.

Words the voice gets wrong

Run every line through Whisper. A period between words ("Sweet. And salty.") fixed a line the voice blurred.

The thumbnail frame

Frame 0 is the cover in many feeds. Start the flash a few frames later so frame 0 stays clean.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Frost, fizz and lightning break up when compressed. Re-encode at 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the crack with the glowing claw. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Credit the brand.

Keep the caption about how it was made, and end it with one line: "Inspired by @brand".

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -maxrate 16M -bufsize 28M -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
