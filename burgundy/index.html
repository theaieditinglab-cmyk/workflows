01 · The idea

Show what's inside, not just the outside.

A phone turning on a gradient looks nice and says nothing. That was the note on the first version: it didn't tell anything. So this film is a guided tour: the outside, the camera, what's inside layer by layer, the chip, the port, the buttons and the colors. Every beat carries one real fact.

Everything you see is a three.js model built from basic shapes in code: no stock, no footage, no 3D file.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

Two things decide a product film: the real facts you'll show and a background that makes the product's color sing.

Facts

Real dimensions (150 × 71.9 × 8.75 mm), the layers, the chip, battery and camera, and the four finishes, collected from the public spec page and teardowns (Sept–Oct 2026) before the script. This is a spec piece about a model that is new at the time: treat the specs shown as illustrative and check them again before you reuse them.

Blush studio, burgundy body, burgundy ink for the accent word, Apple-like grey for sublines.

Type

Inter Tight 700 at 78 px for two- or three-word titles ending in a period, the last word in burgundy, and a 34 px grey subline. Callout cards: 32 px title, 22 px detail, on a light card.

Beats

Hero, macro, layers, chip, rebuild with the port and a 360° spin, fan. Every section starts on a beat of a 120 BPM track.

Pace

Short holds. "The holds feel long" was a real note, so no shot waits more than about two seconds, and callout cards come one at a time, 0.6 s apart.

Format

9:16, 25 s, 30 fps. Titles, cards and the product stay inside the Reels safe zone; only the macros leave the frame, on purpose and without text.

Sound

A warm pad on Cmaj9 – Am9 – Fmaj9 – G6 with plucked arpeggios at 120 BPM, made in code, and SFX of air, metal, glass and precision mechanics. No voice.

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) for your system.

Step 02

Remotion with the 3D stack.

Install Node 22+ and FFmpeg, start a project with npx create-video@latest, then add exact versions that work together: @remotion/three (same version as Remotion), three@0.170.0, @react-three/fiber@8.17.10, @react-three/drei@9.122.0, @types/three@0.170.0.

Step 03

Python for the sound.

numpy and scipy compose the music and every SFX. No sample packs.

Step 04

A GPU-friendly render.

3D renders through Chrome. Pass --gl=angle to every render and still command, or the canvas comes out black.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The film comes from one master prompt. It already includes every fix from the versions that were turned down. The other messages are quality control.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Make a 25-second vertical product film for "iPhone 18 Pro" in Burgundy, in Remotion (React) with real 3D through @remotion/three (three.js + @react-three/fiber + drei). 1080x1920, 30 fps. The music runs at 120 BPM, so 1 beat = 15 frames: every section starts on a beat.

Facts first. Before writing anything, collect the real specs from the product's public spec page and from teardowns (layers, chip, battery, camera, finishes) and list them for me with their source. The film has to tell something true about the phone, not only look pretty. Any number you can't source stays out.

Model. Build the phone in code at real scale, in cm (15.0 x 7.19 x 0.875): rounded aluminum body (corner radius 1.05), a full-width camera plateau, three lenses whose glass is a canvas texture with a 7-blade aperture (meshBasicMaterial, toneMapped false, so the lenses never turn grey), flash, LiDAR and a mic hole, a frosted back glass (fine noise as roughness and bump map), an Apple logo at real size (about 1.9 cm tall), a screen with a lock screen (9:41), the Dynamic Island, side buttons on both sides (Action, volume up and down, power, and Camera Control in dark sapphire), a USB-C port, and speaker and mic grilles. Body color #6E2139, metalness 0.9, roughness 0.22, clearcoat 0.5.
No glitches: keep every surface at least 0.01 away from the one under it (screen at +0.012, Dynamic Island at +0.024, back glass at -0.012), use polygonOffset and alphaTest on every decal (logo, chip label), and a camera with near 1 and far 150.

Light. No HDRI file needed: a drei <Environment> made of <Lightformer> softboxes (a wide top box, two tall side strips for the metal edges, a low bounce, a soft blush ring behind, a front fill that dims during the macro) inside a light grey sphere, plus a narrow strip that sweeps across the phone in the hero, the chip, the spin and the fan. AgX tone mapping, exposure about 1.24. ThreeCanvas with dpr={2}, alpha on, soft shadow from one directional light. Every move eases; spin speed drives a horizontal motion blur.

Background. A blush radial gradient (#F7EEF0 -> #EADADF -> #D9C3CA) with a soft burgundy contact shadow under the phone. Not near-black and not white: the burgundy has to contrast with it.

Type. Inter Tight. One title band at the top of the safe zone (top 268 px): two or three words ending in a period, 78 px, weight 700, ink #1D1D1F with the last word in burgundy #7A1E3A; a grey subline (#6E6E73, 34 px, 500) under it. Words enter one by one with a short blur and rise. The product never sits under text.

Sections (frames at 30 fps):
1. Hero, 0-3 s: the back of the phone floats and turns from three-quarters to the back, with the light strip sweeping across the aluminum. "iPhone 18 Pro." / "Now in Burgundy."
2. Macro, 3-6.7 s: the camera pushes in until three lenses fill the frame; the aperture blades close (at 4.3 s) and open wide (5.6 s), then settle. "Variable aperture." / "48MP Fusion camera."
3. Layers, 6.7-12.5 s: blur cut to the phone lying face up; it explodes layer by layer along its thickness, one layer every 6 frames: front glass, display, vapor chamber, logic board with the chip, battery, aluminum body with the cameras. Five light callout cards appear one at a time, each 0.6 s apart, outside the stack (left and right edges of the safe zone), with a thin burgundy line and a white dot pinned to the 3D point projected to the screen: "Ceramic Shield 2 / 3x more scratch resistant", "Vapor chamber / 3x the surface area", "A20 Pro / First 2 nm chip in iPhone", "Battery / Up to 36 hrs video", "48MP Fusion / Variable aperture". At 11.5 s the camera dives toward the chip on the board. "Layer by layer." / "Engineered from the inside out."
4. Chip, 12.5-15.5 s: cut to a big 3D chip rising from below and turning to face the camera: a dark package with gold pads around it and a lid that reads the logo, "A20" and "PRO", with a soft burgundy glow behind. "A20 Pro." / "The first 2 nm chip in iPhone."
5. Rebuild, 15.5-20.5 s: the layers snap back in a cascade; a close-up on the bottom edge with a label card "USB-C / Speaker and mics" (16.9-17.7 s); then the phone stands up and spins a full 360 degrees showing the buttons on both sides and its screen. "All-day battery." / "Up to 36 hours of video playback."
6. Fan, 20.5-25 s: four phones (Black #2E2E31, Silver #D6D6D8, Glacier #A8C6DB, Burgundy last and on top) rise and open like a hand of cards, spaced in depth so no lens passes through the phone in front. "Four finishes." / "Black, Silver, Glacier and Burgundy." then at 23.5 s "iPhone 18 Pro." / "Available now."
Between sections: a short blur dip (8 frames out, 10 in). No hold longer than about 2 seconds.

Safe zone: every title, card and the product stay inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. The macros may leave the frame on purpose, but with no text on top.

Audio, made in code with numpy/scipy, no samples. Music: warm 120 BPM, a Cmaj9 - Am9 - Fmaj9 - G6 progression in two-second bars; a detuned saw pad whose filter opens with the story, a plucked arpeggio with a 3/16 delay, a light groove (half time in the macro and the spin), a bass line, a soft impact on each section start and a final Cmaj9 chord. SFX few and soft, each on a real event: air as the phone turns, a metallic sheen on the light sweep, a focus servo on the push-in, three tiny blade clicks on the aperture, a glass tink and magnetic releases as the layers separate, a soft key tick for each card, a low hum and a thunk for the chip, magnetic snaps and a solid thunk on the rebuild, a connector click on the USB-C close-up, glass slides as the fan opens, and only one "ting" at the end. Write the music and the SFX as separate stems that add up to the mix; master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB. No narration.

Process. First a 3-second test of the hero at half resolution, rendered with --gl=angle. Then a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with a red safe-zone overlay. Wait for my approval, apply my notes, and only then render the final, with and without sound.

What came back: a reusable phone component (body, plateau, lenses, logo, screen, buttons, port), an exploded version made of six separate layers, a big chip, a camera path per section, a light card layer that follows 3D points, and a Python generator that writes the music, the SFX and the mix.

00:02 · stills before render · claude code
Before rendering, export one still every 1.5 seconds, starting at frame 0, plus one still in the middle of each callout card and of the USB-C close-up, all with the red safe-zone outline (left 90, right 960, top 250, bottom 1480 px), in one contact sheet. Render them with --gl=angle. For each still tell me: any text or product outside the zone, any callout card over a part, any flicker or surface showing through another (look closely at the logo, the screen edge and the fan), any lens that went grey, any title that stays less than 1.5 seconds, and whether the buttons and the USB-C port are visible where they should be.

What came back: a contact sheet with the safe zone drawn on it. This is where the 3D problems show up: surfaces flickering through each other, lenses passing through the phone in front in the fan, cards sitting on top of parts.

Approved. Render the final at 1080x1920, 30 fps, CRF 16, with --gl=angle and concurrency 3: one MP4 with sound and one without. Then re-encode both at a constant 14 Mbps, and give me the music, the SFX and the mix as separate WAV files. Also export a cover still from the fan at 22.3 s with the "Four finishes." title.
05 · What Claude built

A phone made of code.

MODEL

Real scale, real details

Units in centimeters. Extruded rounded rectangles for the body and plateau, cylinders for lenses and flash, capsules for the buttons and the USB-C port, rows of small holes for the speaker and mics. The lens glass is a canvas texture that redraws the 7 aperture blades for each value.

LIGHT

A studio without an HDRI

Lightformer softboxes inside a drei Environment give the aluminum its long highlights. One narrow strip moves across the phone on each big moment: that's the light sweep.

LABELS

3D points, 2D cards

Each callout is pinned to a point on its layer. The code projects that point with the same camera as the scene and draws the dot, the line and the card as normal HTML on top, so the text stays sharp.

SOUND

Composed in code

A pad and plucks on a warm progression, with each SFX written at the exact frame of its event: blade clicks on the aperture, magnetic snaps on the rebuild, glass slides on the fan, one ting at the end.

key code · the exploded layers · tsx
// layers: assembled z -> exploded z (along the phone's thickness, in cm)
const LAYERS = [
  {id: 'glass',   z0: 0.47, z1: 7.0},
  {id: 'display', z0: 0.38, z1: 4.7},
  {id: 'vapor',   z0: 0.24, z1: 2.35},
  {id: 'board',   z0: 0.12, z1: 0.0},
  {id: 'battery', z0: 0.0,  z1: -1.6},
  {id: 'tray',    z0: 0,    z1: -7.3},
] as const;
const explodeAmt = (f: number, i: number) => {
  if (f >= 465) return 1 - k(f, 470 + (5 - i) * 4, 490 + (5 - i) * 4, inOut); // rebuild, in cascade
  return k(f, 208 + i * 6, 238 + i * 6, inOut);                                // one layer every 6 frames
};

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

Tell something true

The rejected version only spun the phone. The approved one walks through what's inside, with one sourced fact per beat.

02

A background with contrast

Near-black and then white both lost information. Blush keeps the burgundy rich and every edge readable.

03

One card at a time

Callouts sit on light cards outside the parts and appear one by one while their layer is in focus, so you can read each one.

04

Give the detail its moment

A port seen for half a second in a spin doesn't exist. The USB-C gets its own close-up and its own label.

05

A fan, not a row

Four phones side by side looked like a catalog. Opened like a hand of cards, with the hero color on top, they look like an ad.

06

Quiet sound

Few, soft SFX tied to real events, and a single ting at the very end. Every section lands on a beat of the music.

08 · Watch out

What went wrong, and the fix.

Flicker while spinning

Z-fighting: two surfaces too close fight for the same pixels. Keep every surface at least 0.01 from the one below, use polygonOffset and alphaTest on decals, and set the camera near to 1 (and far as tight as the scene allows; the notes say 150).

The "Blender look"

Flat default lighting makes a coded model look like a test render. Studio softboxes (Lightformers), AgX tone mapping, a frosted back glass and dpr 2 fixed most of it.

A tiny logo

The first logo was too small and read as a mistake. Draw it at real size, about 1.9 cm tall on a 15 cm phone.

No buttons, no port

A phone without buttons or a charging port looked fake. Add both sides' buttons, the USB-C port and the grilles, and show them up close.

Too dark, then too white

A very dark background followed by a white one lost detail on both. Pick one background that contrasts with the product color and keep it.

Colors in a row

Four phones in a line looked worse than a fan. In the fan, lenses of the back phones went through the phone in front until they were spaced further apart in depth.

Grey lenses

Lenses turned grey when the phone turned, because they reflected the studio. Draw the lens glass with an unlit material that ignores tone mapping.

Unreadable callouts

Labels written on top of the parts couldn't be read. Move them to light cards outside the stack, linked by a thin line.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Renderers often come out at 5–6 Mbps on flat designs, and Instagram compresses again. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the product best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the headline and the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
