01 · The idea

Same shape, 50 years apart.

Car ads in code usually fail for one reason: the car is built from boxes and looks like a toy. So this one starts from two real 3D models, and the story is the most famous thing about the 911: its shape never left. A plane of light sweeps the first 911 Turbo and leaves today's Turbo S behind it, in the same spot.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. Porsche does not allow its crest or its logotype in third-party ads, so the crests and plates were removed, the name appears as plain text, and the car's own rear lettering only shows, lit like a sign, in the two rear shots.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

The most-watched car reels

3D car showcases with a single color world, a mirror floor and slow pushes on details (headlights, wheels, wing), and Porsche's own configurator reel where the wheels change on a static car. Real details, real light, cuts on the beat.

Real models, real license

Two CC BY 4.0 models: the 1975 911 Turbo by Karol Miklas and the 911 Turbo S by n.brizitskaya, found on public GitHub mirrors with their license files. Models posted from racing games were skipped even when labeled CC BY.

Brand rules

Porsche's trademark page: no use of its marks without written permission. So no crest and no logotype of ours: the name in plain text and the trademark notice in the caption. The only brand lettering is the car's own, on its tail, in two shots.

Palette

Porsche's digital look is black, white and gray; the color comes from the cars. A warm graphite studio, a red 930, a silver 992 and the amber of the light.

Facts

From Porsche's own pages: the 911 Turbo premiered at the Paris Motor Show in 1974 with 260 PS; the 2025 911 Turbo S has 711 PS, does 0–100 km/h in 2.5 s and lapped the Nordschleife in 7:03.92.

Sound

A trailer cue made in code whose bass is a synthesized flat-six, with its rpm following the picture: start, idle, blips, launch with gear shifts, a last rev.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 24-second vertical spec ad for the Porsche 911 Turbo called "Turbo. 50 years." in Remotion (React) with three.js through @remotion/three, 1080x1920 at 30 fps. The idea: the 911 Turbo was born in 1974 and its shape never left. So the ad uses two real 3D cars, a 1975 911 Turbo (930) and a 911 Turbo S (992), and tells the story with light. Use real models, never cars built from boxes.

Models: load two CC BY 4.0 glTF models with GLTFLoader (meshopt decoder for the compressed one): "FREE 1975 Porsche 911 (930) Turbo" by Karol Miklas and "Porsche 911 with interior" by n.brizitskaya. Normalize both to meters at their real length, length along X, nose toward +X, wheels on y = 0, centered on the paint. Hide the crests, license plates and the air freshener. Cut the chrome rear lettering out of the 992's chrome material into its own mesh, so it can be lit like a sign only in the two rear shots. Replace the body paint with a MeshPhysicalMaterial car paint: clearcoat 1, clearcoat roughness 0.025, metalness 0.55 and a fine noise normal map for metallic flake. Guards-red-like on the 930, silver on the 992. Split the 992's four wheels (rim, tire and disc triangles near each axle) into their own meshes with a pivot on the axle, so they spin by distance / radius.

Look: a warm graphite car studio (a screen-space gradient with a soft warm halo behind the car, darker at the top so white headlines read) with a translucent gray floor and a mirrored copy of each car under it for the reflection, environment lighting only (drei Environment with Lightformers: a top softbox, two long side strips, a warm rim), ACES tone mapping at exposure 1.45, dpr 1.5. The tunnel and the track stay near-black so their lights shine. Type: Inter only, from a local file loaded with FontFace. Porsche does not allow its crest or logotype in third-party ads, so the name appears as plain text in Inter. Only show a number under a headline when it adds something (a counting year, PS, a distance); never repeat the headline in small type.

Scenes, one idea per 3 seconds, each starting with its headline and the car already in frame:
1. 0-3s: "1974. The first 911 Turbo." Macro of the 930's round headlight; the lamp switches on (emissive + glow sprite) and the camera flies back to a 3/4 view.
2. 3-6s: "Same shape. 50 years later." A plane of light sweeps the car from tail to nose. Two clipping planes follow it: behind the plane it is already the 992, in front it is still the 930. A year counter runs 1975 → 2025.
3. 6-9s: "Rear engine. Since 1963." Rear 3/4 of the 992, an orange light under the engine cover glows through the grille and the rear lettering lights up.
4. 9-12s: "0–100 km/h." A tunnel of light strips that stream past (the car stands still, the world moves), dynamic environment so the reflections run over the body, wheels spinning, a live counter that reaches 100 km/h at exactly 2.50 s.
5. 12-15s: "711 PS. The most powerful 911 yet." Low camera at the front, the light strips stretch with speed, a counter from 260 PS (1974) to 711 PS.
6. 15-18s: "One lap. 7:03.92" The camera rises from the roof to a top-down view while the 992 drives one lap of the real Nürburgring Nordschleife (OpenStreetMap relation 38566, credited on screen) and the track draws itself in light behind it, with a lap timer and "Nürburgring · 20.8 km".
7. 18-21s: "One light. Edge to edge." Macro of the rear light bar, lit from the center out to the edges with a shader mask on its emissive, with the lettering glowing under it.
8. 21-24s: "Porsche 911 Turbo. Since 1974." Both cars side by side on the mirror floor, a light sweeping across them.

Every number on screen must come from Porsche's newsroom or investor relations. Keep every headline, label and car inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480 (only macros may leave on purpose, with no text over them).

Audio: an original track made in code (numpy): a hybrid film-trailer cue in C minor at 120 BPM in 3/4, so each block is two bars (taiko, low strings, a pulsing ostinato, braams on the big moments). The bass is the engine: a clean flat-six synthesized from its firing orders (3, 6, 9) with a little half-order burble, two exhaust resonances, almost no noise and gentle saturation, louder only under load, its rpm tied to the picture (starts with the headlight, idles on G, blips on the beat at the rear, launches with real gear shifts in the tunnel, fades on the track, revs at the end). One sound per event: relay click, the air of the sweeping light, hot metal ticks, a tire chirp at launch, light strips passing, a doppler pass, a humming pen of light for the track, the light bar opening in stereo. Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice af_heart: "The very first nine-eleven Turbo." / "Fifty years later, the same shape." / "The engine still lives in the back." / "Zero to a hundred in two and a half seconds." / "The most powerful nine-eleven yet." / "One full lap. Seven minutes, three seconds." / "One light. Edge to edge." / "Nine-eleven Turbo. Since nineteen seventy-four." Check every line with Whisper. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked, plus a copy without the outline. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: one Remotion composition with both cars loaded once, a camera path per block, the swap with two clipping planes, the tunnel, the track and the light bar, with the music, the engine, the SFX and the narration on the timeline.

05 · What Claude built

A story told with light.

SWAP

Two cars, one plane of light

Both models share the same spot. Two clipping planes ride an additive sheet of light: behind it you see the 992, in front of it the 930, and a year counter runs with it.

TUNNEL

The car stands still, the world moves

Light strips stream past and stretch with speed, the environment re-renders every frame so reflections run over the paint, and the wheels turn by the distance driven.

LAP

A real lap, drawn in light

The Nordschleife comes from OpenStreetMap: 53 segments chained into one 20.7 km line. The 992 drives it and the ribbon of light draws itself behind the car while the camera rises from the roof.

SOUND

The engine is the bass

A flat-six synthesized from crank orders, idling on the dominant of C minor, launching with gear shifts on the same speed curve as the counter. Mix at −14 LUFS, true peak −2.1 dB.

// the wheels: cut out of the model, spun by the distance driven

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Models that load after the frame

A model that arrives through fetch needs the 3D canvas advanced once before you continue the render, or the still comes out without the car.

Badges hide in other materials

Hiding the logo material is not enough: the lettering on the tail lived in the chrome material. Check the tail and the hood in close-up.

Reflections in a render pipeline

A real-time mirror material may not refresh frame by frame. A mirrored copy of the car under a translucent black floor is cheap and always works.

Lines that flicker far away

A ribbon a few millimeters above the floor fights with it for depth when the camera is 300 m up. Lift it and draw it after the floor.

Names the voice can't say

Text-to-speech read "Nordschleife" as something else and Whisper caught it. Rewrite the line instead of fighting the voice.

An engine that growls

A strong half order plus combustion noise and heavy saturation sounds hoarse, not like a flat-six. Build it from the firing orders, add exhaust resonances and keep it quiet at idle.

Numbers nobody knows

"Nordschleife" alone meant nothing to most viewers. Show what the number is: the car drives the lap while the timer runs.

Temp files that fill the disk

Every new bundle copies your whole public folder. Bundle once per batch of stills or chunks and delete it when you finish.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Reflections on dark paint band easily when compressed. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the most striking frame (here, the car half old and half new). The profile grid crops covers to 3:4, so keep the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the credits.

"Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]", the trademark notice, the CC BY credit for each model and the OpenStreetMap credit.

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
