01 · The idea

Same bricks, new story.

A LEGO set is never finished. So the ad starts on something almost done, a minifig puts in the last brick, and that brick sets off a new build. The point is not one brick in a studio: it is the moment of playing, brick by brick, with the logo inside the world.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab with real LEGO parts, the official logo and the brand's colors.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

A LEGO ad only works if the bricks look like real bricks and you can watch them being placed.

Real parts

The LDraw library has the exact geometry of every LEGO part and models of real sets. three.js loads them with LDrawLoader, so nothing is invented.

Brickfilm

Bricks move at 12 frames per second like stop motion, the camera moves smoothly, and there is a macro blur, so it feels like someone is playing.

Palette

LEGO red, yellow and blue on a sea of studs, with the official logo on real bricks.

#E3000B#FFD500#0055BF#1E6FD0#4B9F4A#E4CD9E
A character

The pilot minifig from the seaplane set gets joints: it runs with the logo, throws the last brick, flies through the explosion and ends up waving from the plane.

Pace

One idea and one headline about every 3 seconds, the build already moving on frame 0, and a slow-motion beat where the bricks blow apart.

Format

9:16, 27 s, 30 fps, with the minifig, every headline and the logo inside the Reels safe zone.

Sound

Sound that tells the story: a car that starts, revs and honks as it is built, a ship horn when the lighthouse lights up, the same melody coming back on brass after the explosion, bricks that play the tune as they land, a seaplane engine up front, and a seven-line narration timed to the on-screen words.

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) for your system.

Step 02

Install Node 22+ and FFmpeg.

Start a Remotion project with npx create-video@latest, add @remotion/three, three, @react-three/fiber, @react-three/drei and @react-three/postprocessing, and open Claude Code inside that folder.

Step 03

Get the LEGO parts.

Download the packed LDraw models from the three.js repository (examples/models/ldraw/officialLibrary) into public/. They are CC BY 2.0: credit LDraw.org in the video or the caption.

Step 04

Python and a GPU-friendly render.

numpy and scipy compose the music and SFX, and Kokoro TTS reads the narration. Pass --gl=angle to every render and still command, or the canvas comes out black.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: a half-resolution test, stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 27-second vertical spec ad for LEGO called "The Last Brick" in Remotion (React) with @remotion/three, 1080x1920 at 30 fps. Use REAL LEGO sets, not bricks made up in code: load official parts from the LDraw library (CC BY 2.0) with three.js's LDrawLoader. Use the packed models in the three.js examples: the car, the lighthouse (30023) and the Island Hopper seaplane (5935). Render with --gl=angle. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

The story, in one continuous world: a minifig finishes a little coastal street (a lighthouse and a red car), throws in the last brick, everything blows apart, and the same bricks rebuild into a seaplane that takes off.

Pieces: keep every LDraw part as its own object with its original matrix, so each brick can fly on its own. Keep the LDraw edge lines at half opacity so every brick reads as a brick. Replace the materials with glossy ABS plastic. Pieces move at 12 frames per second like a stop-motion brickfilm; the camera moves smoothly at 30. Every brick lands the LEGO way: it arrives aligned above its spot, drops, presses and bounces a little.

The minifig: use the pilot from set 5935 (head, torso, arms, hands, hips, legs), with pivot groups for the legs, arms and head, so it can run, hold things up, throw and wave. Load a second copy of the model for it, because a three.js object can only live in one group.

Scenes:
1. 0-5.5s "Almost done." with a counter "41 pieces left": the top of the car and the lighthouse build themselves brick by brick on camera, layer by layer, from frame 0. The minifig runs toward the camera holding the official LEGO logo up on a red 4x4 tile, cheers, and clicks the tile onto the road.
2. 4.4-5.6s: it grabs the last brick, throws it in an arc and the brick clicks onto the lighthouse. The music stops dead just before. The lamp lights up and its beam sweeps across the camera with a flash.
3. 6.3-8.4s "Or just getting started.": the whole street breaks apart from the top down, in slow motion (0.35x, then catching up). The minifig flies through the bricks; a few bricks brush past the camera.
4. 8.4-16.3s "Same bricks." / "New story.": the same bricks fly in and rebuild the Island Hopper, wings and tail first so it reads as a plane at once, then layer by layer. Each old brick becomes the new brick closest in color. The logo tile becomes the tail fin, printed with the logo. The minifig lands sitting on top of the plane and waves.
5. 17.4-22s "Up we go.": the plane taxis on a sea of studs, lifts off with a splash ring and flies right over the camera.
6. 22.2-27s: a red 3D brick with the logo printed on top drops onto the studs and clicks in with a shockwave. "Build anything." sits right under it; the spec ad line and the LDraw credit go small at the bottom.

On every big click (the logo tile, the last brick, the explosion, the pilot landing, the finished plane, the takeoff, the final logo) add a white shockwave ring, a few flying studs and a short camera shake. The sky is a dome with a direction gradient (not a screen background) and the sea is a plane with a repeated stud texture, so there is no hard horizon. Keep every headline, the minifig and the logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. One idea per block of about 3 seconds.

Audio: an original calypso track made in code (numpy) in D major at 128 BPM with a synthesized steel pan (drum modes plus a rubber mallet), brass stabs, congas, a seed shaker and a round bass. Export the event times from the scene (every brick landing, every big click) and put one sound on each: a plastic click per brick that rises in pitch with its height, the minifig's footsteps, a whistle for the thrown brick, a hum and a glass ping for the lamp, a deep boom and a rattle for the explosion, an engine that coughs and speeds up, a splash and a flyover. Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice am_puck at 1.12x: "Okay. Last few pieces!" / "Now... the final one!" / "Or is it?" / "Same bricks. Brand-new ride." / "Let's fly!" / "The LEGO brick. Build anything!" Check every line with Whisper. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: first render the opening at half resolution and check it at 10 frames per second. Then export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked, and mark where the logo appears. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: one Remotion composition with one three.js world: the LDraw sets split into single bricks, the minifig with joints, the brick-by-brick build with its counter, the explosion in slow motion, the rebuild into the seaplane, the takeoff and the logo brick, plus an event file that the audio script reads to put one sound on every brick.

00:02 · stills before render · claude code
Before rendering, export one still every 1.5 seconds, starting at frame 0, at half resolution with a red outline marking the Instagram Reels safe zone (left 90, right 960, top 250, bottom 1480 px). Put them in one contact sheet and mark every frame where the LEGO logo appears. Then pull the explosion and the rebuild again at 10 frames per second. Tell me if you can see each brick being placed, if the logo ever hides behind something or sits still in front of the action, if the plane reads as a plane from the first pieces, and if the minifig, the logo or the text leave the zone.

What came back: the first cut used bricks made up in code and nine separate scenes, so there was no story and the bricks did not read. Real sets, a build you can watch and a single world fixed it. The stills also caught a logo sign that covered the explosion, a second logo on the plane, a plane that looked like a boat at first and a pilot hidden inside a closed cockpit. All of it was fixed before the final render.

05 · What Claude built

Real bricks, a real click.

PARTS

LDraw, brick by brick

Each part of the car, the lighthouse and the seaplane is its own object with its original place saved, so it can be built, blown apart and rebuilt one brick at a time.

MINIFIG

Eight parts, five joints

The pilot from the seaplane set, with pivots at the hips, shoulders and neck. It runs, holds the logo up, throws, tumbles through the air and waves.

REBUILD

Same bricks, new set

Every brick of the street is matched to the seaplane brick closest in color, so the new build grows out of the old one. The logo tile becomes the tail.

SOUND

A click per brick

The scene writes the time of every landing to a file and the audio script reads it. Each plane brick plays a note of the chord, the music steps back when the car and the plane speak, and the logo closes on the same click as the first brick.

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

Real sets, not look-alikes

Bricks made up in code looked flat. Real LDraw parts, with their edge lines, read as LEGO at a glance.

02

Watch it being built

A finished model says nothing. Starting almost done and placing the last bricks on camera is what makes it feel like play.

03

The logo lives in the world

A logo on a sign in front of the camera hid the action. On a red tile in the minifig's hands, on the tail and on the last brick, it is part of the story.

04

One beat of slow motion

The explosion runs at a third of the speed and then catches up, so you can see the minifig flying through the bricks.

05

Wings first

The rebuild starts with the wings and the tail, so it reads as a plane from the first bricks and not as a boat.

06

Stills before render

Rounds of stills, plus the fast parts at 10 frames per second, caught every problem in minutes.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

A black canvas

With async models, the 3D canvas needs preserveDrawingBuffer and one forced frame after loading, or the stills come out empty.

A sky glued to the screen

A background texture stays fixed to the screen and leaves a hard line at the horizon. Use a sky dome that follows the direction.

Closed cockpits

If the set's cockpit is closed, the pilot disappears. Sit the minifig on top, next to something worth showing.

One object, one group

A three.js object can only be in one place. Load a second copy of the model for the character.

Text over bricks

White headlines over light bricks get lost. Add a soft dark gradient behind the headline and a strong shadow.

Brand names in TTS

"Lego. Build anything" was heard as "Let go". "The LEGO brick. Build anything!" reads clearly. Check every line with Whisper.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Thousands of studs turn to mush at low bitrates. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the explosion with the minifig in the air and the official logo on top. The feed crops covers to 4:5 and the profile grid to 3:4, so keep everything between y 300 and 1480.

Step 04

A short caption.

What the brand's style is, that it was made with Claude Code, the keyword, and end with "Inspired by @brand".

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 7M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -

Before you post: everything inside x 90–960 and y 250–1480 · the logo in the scene at least every 6 seconds · no text on screen for less than 1.5 s · something moves on frame 0 · −14 LUFS · 12–16 Mbps.
