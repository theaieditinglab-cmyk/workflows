01 · The idea

One candy, one accident, one party.

M&M’s ads have always been about the candies as characters. So the spot starts with one of them bored on a sofa, the bag is the accident that sets everything off, and the last line puts the party back where it came from: in the bag.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab with the official logo, the brand’s colors and a bag drawn in code.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

A candy ad only works if the candies look good enough to eat and the packaging behaves like real plastic.

The candy

A lentil shape with a glossy shell and the white “m” printed on top. Built in code, so it can be repeated 300 times without a single model file.

The bag

Drawn on a canvas and placed on a soft mesh. It puffs, bends, wobbles and crumples, and when it rips the hole is real.

Palette

The six candy colors on a milk chocolate brown, with the official logo on the bag.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: a half-resolution test, stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 20-second vertical spec ad for M&M's called "The Party Is in the Bag" in Remotion (React) with @remotion/three, 1080x1920 at 30 fps, all in code: the candies, the bag, the room and the music. Render with --gl=angle. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

The story, in one continuous world: on a Friday night a red M&M's candy is bored on a huge sofa. It bounces on the cushion until the bag next to it falls over and rips open, 300 candies pour out, and the living room turns into a disco. At the end everyone jumps back into the bag: the party is in the bag.

The candies: a lentil shape (a squashed sphere), glossy candy-shell material with a clearcoat, and the white "m" printed on top as a decal that follows the curve. Six official colors. Use InstancedMesh, one per color, so 300 candies render fast. The hero is a red candy with the same shape, never a different character.

The bag: draw the front of the bag on a canvas (brown, the official logo, "milk chocolate", the row of candies) and put it on a plane with 60x34 segments. Deform its vertices every frame so it is soft: it puffs when the red one bounces, bends like a tortilla when it falls, wobbles like jelly when it lands and crumples a little. When it rips, cut a real jagged hole in the texture (transparent, with a silver plastic edge) and push real 3D candies out through it. Never a flat sticker of a rip.

Physics: precompute the spill with cannon-es in a Node script (300 bodies, 120 Hz, floor, sofa and walls) and save positions and rotations to a binary file. In Remotion, read the pose for each frame. Slow the spill down to 0.35x for 0.6 seconds right after the rip, then catch up.

Scenes:
1. 0-1.5s "Friday night.": a close-up of the red candy sunk into the cushion, cold moonlight, a ticking clock, everything else out of focus.
2. 1.5-3.1s: it starts bouncing, the camera pulls back fast, the bag wobbles on the next cushion and falls to the floor on its side.
3. 3.1-6s "Call the crew.": the bag rips in slow motion and the candies pour across the floor.
4. 6-10s: the party takes us somewhere else: the sofa, the clock, the lamp and the window sink into the floor, and the wall flips tile by tile from the center into a dark LED wall in the candy colors that pulses in waves on every beat. The floor lights up tile by tile, a mirror disco ball made of 1,100 tiles comes down, colored spotlights sweep in and confetti falls. From second 7 the candies already jump on the beat.
5. 10-15s: the party: everyone dances on the tiles, the camera orbits.
6. 15-17s: everyone jumps back into a big bag in an arc, one after another.
7. 17-20s "The party is in the bag.": the bag glows with the party light still coming out of its mouth, and the official logo is on it.

Keep every headline, the candies that matter and the logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. The logo (on the bag) is in the scene from the start, in the middle and at the end. One idea per block of about 3 seconds.

Audio: an original track made in code (numpy) at 120 BPM in F minor: a sad muted trombone for the boredom, a reverse rise in the slow motion, and a disco drop at 6 seconds with four-on-the-floor kick, offbeat hats, a funky bass and bright arpeggios. Sounds tied to events: a ticking clock, crickets, cushion hops, the plastic rip, one tiny click for each candy that hits the floor (timed with the slow motion), a blip for each tile that lights up, chimes for the disco ball, pops when they jump into the bag. Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice af_heart: "Friday night, nothing to do." / "Unless?" / "Call the crew." / "Now we're talking." / "And when it's time to go..." / "The party is in the bag." Check every line with Whisper, duck the music under the voice and master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: first render the opening at half resolution and check it at 10 frames per second. Then export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked, and mark where the logo appears. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: one Remotion composition with one three.js world: the sofa and the room, the hero candy, the soft bag with its rip, the physics of 300 candies read from a precomputed file, the light-up floor, the mirror ball, the colored spotlights and the return to the bag, plus the scripts for the music and the sound.

00:02 · stills before render · claude code
Before rendering, export one still every 1.5 seconds, starting at frame 0, at half resolution with a red outline marking the Instagram Reels safe zone (left 90, right 960, top 250, bottom 1480 px). Put them in one contact sheet and mark every frame where the M&M's logo appears. Then pull the fall and the rip again at 10 frames per second. Tell me if the bag looks soft, if the rip is a real hole with candies coming out of it (not a drawing on top), if the hero candy stays the same character, and if any frame has black blocks or flickers.

What came back: the first bag was a rigid box and the rip was a flat drawing of a hole with candies painted on it. A mesh that deforms and a real hole cut out of the texture, with 3D candies pushing through, fixed it. The stills also caught a disco ball made of candies (the hero cannot be a prop), a flat start and party lights that switched off too early. All of it was fixed before the final render.

00:03 · final render · claude code
Approved. Render the final at 1080x1920 and 30 fps with --gl=angle and --concurrency=1 (the post-processing can leave black blocks with more). Check every frame for sudden drops in brightness, re-render only the bad stretch if any, re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with and without sound, and give me the music, SFX, voice and mix as separate WAV files.
05 · What Claude built

A soft bag and real physics.

BAG

2,000 vertices that move

The front and back of the bag are two meshes whose points are recalculated every frame: puffed, bent, jelly-like on landing, crumpled on impact.

RIP

A real hole

The rip is cut out of the bag’s own texture in three stages, with a silver plastic edge, and seven 3D candies push their way out through it.

PHYSICS

300 candies, precomputed

cannon-es simulates the spill once in Node at 120 Hz and saves every pose. Remotion only reads them, so every render is identical.

SOUND

A click per candy

The audio script knows when each candy touches the floor, including the slow motion, and puts a tiny click on it, on top of a disco track made in code.

key code · three.js
// a soft bag: the vertices move every frame
z  = depth * bulgeX * bulgeY;                                  // the puffed pillow shape
z += jelly * 0.9 * sin(PI * u) * sin(PI * v);                  // wobbles like jelly when it lands
z += crumple * 0.5 * (noise(u * 7, v * 5) - 0.5);              // crumples on impact
y += bend * (u - 0.5) ** 2 * width * 0.9;                      // bends like a tortilla

// the rip is a real hole: cut it out of the texture, then push 3D candies through
ctx.globalCompositeOperation = 'destination-out'; jaggedHole(ctx, 1.0); ctx.fill();

// cannon-es ran once in Node; Remotion only reads the pose
const simT = t => t < 3.1 ? t : t < 3.7 ? 3.1 + (t - 3.1) * 0.35 : 3.31 + (t - 3.7);   // slow motion

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

Start close and moody

A wide shot of a sofa says nothing. A close-up of the bored candy, with moonlight and shallow focus, makes you care in one second.

02

Packaging is soft

A rigid bag looked like a box. Plastic that puffs, bends and wobbles sells the product before you see a single candy.

03

A real rip

A drawing of a hole looks like a sticker. A hole cut out of the texture, with candies coming through it, reads at once.

04

One beat of slow motion

The spill runs at a third of the speed for half a second and then catches up, so you can see the bag open.

05

The hero is never a prop

A disco ball made of candies broke the story. A mirror ball keeps the candies as characters.

06

Stills before render

Rounds of stills, plus the fall and the rip at 10 frames per second, caught every problem in minutes.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Black blocks

Post-processing with multisampling left black rectangles in some frames. Turn multisampling off, render with one thread and check every frame’s brightness.

Light that does nothing

With a scene environment, a material’s envMapIntensity is ignored. Give the material its own envMap or use environmentIntensity.

A flat rip

A decal of a hole on top of the bag looks pasted on. Cut the hole out of the bag’s texture instead.

Physics that changes

Simulating inside the render gives a different spill every time. Precompute it once and read the poses.

Text over candies

White headlines over bright candies get lost. Use a thick dark outline and a soft gradient behind them.

Brand names in TTS

Write “M and M’s” in the narration script and check every line with Whisper.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Confetti and hundreds of shiny candies turn to mush at low bitrates. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Candies flying over the disco room, with the official logo big in the center and the tagline below. The feed crops covers to 4:5 and the profile grid to 3:4, so keep everything between y 300 and 1480.

Step 04

A short caption.

What the brand’s style is, that it was made with Claude Code, the keyword, and end with “Inspired by @brand”.

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 7M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -

Before you post: everything inside x 90–960 and y 250–1480 · the logo in the scene at least every 6 seconds · no text on screen for less than 1.5 s · something moves on frame 0 · −14 LUFS · 12–16 Mbps.
