01 · The idea

Sending money should feel like sending a text.

Money apps usually get ads that cut between five screens. Here the lime flag of the Wise logo and its line do every job: they reveal the logo, underline the amount, rise into a rate chart, fly from New York to Bogotá, fill the progress bar and branch out into 40+ currencies. The viewer follows one thing, so five features read as one story: type it, see the rate, watch it fly, it arrived.

It is a spec piece. The Wise logo is Wise's official asset; the film was designed and built by the Lab.

05 · What Claude built

A spot that is actually a web page.

STRUCTURE

Seven HyperFrames scenes

A root index.html places seven compositions on the timeline. Each one is its own HTML file with a GSAP timeline that HyperFrames steps through frame by frame, so every render comes out identical.

NUMBERS

Real slot machines

Every digit is a column of numbers that spins and lands on its value, each one a little later than the one before, so the amount reads left to right.

MUSIC

Composed in code

Light funk, synthesized from scratch in Python, with the tempo tuned so the first slam and the final logo landing fall exactly on a beat. No samples from the brand.

SOUND

Money-app SFX

Keypad taps, rate ticks that slow down, the flight, the pin's "tock", a two-note "arrived" chime, the card flip and the currency chips.

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

The logo does the work

The lime flag is the brand's own shape, so it becomes the thread. Every scene grows out of its line, and the spot never feels like a list of features.

02

A dark stage, the brand's green as light

On deep forest green, the bright green reads like light. White app cards pop without a single gradient.

03

Every claim has a source

Rate, speed and currencies were checked on the brand's own pages, and the caption says where and when. In a money ad, one wrong number undoes everything else.

04

Promise less, show more

"≈" and "before fees" in small print instead of an exact payout. The animation does the persuading, not the fine print.

05

It ends on their logo

An earlier cut closed on our own card. The final ends on Wise, like a real spot would. Our name lives in the caption.

06

A sound of its own

Light funk, not the house of our first Wise piece or the marimba of Duolingo. Each brand gets a sonic identity, and the beat lands on the logo.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Verify every claim

Check the rate, the number of currencies, the countries and the speed on the brand's own pages before they go on screen. Put the source and date in the caption.

Keep the logo untouched

If you split the mark from the wordmark, check that the next path doesn't start with a relative "m" command, or a letter will shift.

Frame 0 matters

Start with the mark already flying in, never an empty screen. The first frame is the first impression and often the cover.

Use the current logo

Wise's site still hosts its old blue logo under wise.com/public-resources/assets/logos/wise/. The current one is .../logos/wise-personal/logo.svg (and logo_inverse.svg for dark stages).

Spell for the voice, not the screen

Kokoro TTS mispronounces "Bogotá" with the accent. Write "Bogota" in the narration text only and keep the accent on screen.

Money stays honest

Freeze the figures on a date, label them "before fees · illustrative" and promise no fees or delivery times you can't back.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Renderers often come out at 5–6 Mbps on flat designs, and Instagram compresses again. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the product best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the headline and the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
