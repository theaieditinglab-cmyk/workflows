01 · The idea

One voice, a new material every 3 seconds.

A voice company sells something you can't see. So the spot makes a voice visible the way physics does: sound moves sand, water, light and fire. The narration is analysed first, and every scene reads those numbers, so what you see really follows what you hear.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. No footage, logo or sound of the brand was used, and the narration is an open-source voice.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

Nigel Stanford's "CYMATICS: Science Vs. Music" passed 54M views with one experiment per beat: salt on a Chladni plate, water on a speaker, a Rubens' tube of flames, all macro on near-black. Ten-second Chladni loops still reach 8M views in 2026.

Sound you can see

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 27-second vertical spec ad for ElevenLabs called "Every voice has a shape." in Remotion (React), 1080x1920 at 30 fps. The idea: one real voice drives every frame. Every 3 seconds the same voice shapes a different material, like a cymatics experiment: sand, light, water, rock, fire, vinyl and frost.

The voice first: write one narration line per block, generate it with Kokoro TTS (voice af_heart) and place each line at the start of its block. Then analyse the placed track in Python and save it for the scenes: loudness per frame (RMS, normalised), a log-frequency spectrogram as a grayscale PNG (96 bands from 80 Hz to 8 kHz, one column per frame) and the peak waveform of one line (1,200 signed points). Every visual reads from those numbers, so it moves with the real voice.

Scenes, one idea per 3 seconds:
1. 0-3s: "Every voice has a shape." A black brushed-metal plate with 30,000 grains of sand already bouncing at frame 0. Each word switches to a new Chladni mode, f = cos(n·pi·x)·cos(m·pi·y) ± cos(m·pi·x)·cos(n·pi·y), and every grain slides to the nodal lines with Newton steps (x -= f·grad f / |grad f|²). Grains jump harder when the voice is louder.
2. 3-6s: TEXT TO SPEECH. "Type a line, and hear it." types itself on a light canvas; then every pixel of the text flies off in an arc and lands as the real waveform of "and hear it", and an orange playhead scrubs across it.
3. 6-9s: "Whisper it." / "Shout it." A phosphor oscilloscope in XY mode: the whisper is a tiny trembling ellipse, the shout is a huge 3:2 Lissajous figure. Draw the beam as three additive strokes plus five fading copies of past instants for phosphor persistence. The audio of this block is the figure itself: left channel = x, right channel = y.
4. 9-12s: "90+ languages." A top-down dish of dark water in a WebGL2 fragment shader: standing waves from N directions reflect a ring light, the classic cymatics look. Ten greetings (Hello, Hola, Bonjour…) appear around the rim one by one and stay; each one changes the symmetry N and the wave number.
5. 12-15s: "10,000+ voices. Or clone your own." A raymarched heightfield: the camera flies low through a canyon whose walls are the voice's spectrogram, at a pastel sunrise with fog.
6. 15-18s: "Describe a sound." with a typed prompt pill, "a crackling fire, up close". A brass Rubens' tube with 34 flames in a shader; flame heights follow a standing wave set by the voice.
7. 18-21s: "Or a whole song." A macro of a record in a shader: grooves that wobble with music and an anisotropic sheen, pulling out from inside a groove to the whole spinning record with an orange label.
8. 21-24s: "It listens, too. Speech to text." Frost grows across dark glass and writes "word for word." first inside the letters, then everywhere around them.
9. 24-27s: A light canvas with soft pastel glows: dark sand settles into a final figure, "ElevenLabs" as text, "Every voice has a shape." and the disclaimer.

Brand rules: write "ElevenLabs" as one word and use it only as text, in the same typeface as the rest; no logo or icon anywhere. Headlines in Inter Tight (light and regular weights, tight tracking; the brand's own display face is commercial). Their facts only from elevenlabs.io docs. Keep every headline and every object inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480.

Audio: an original track at 120 BPM in F sharp minor made only of synthesized voices: a formant choir (a glottal pulse through three vowel filters), a hummed bass, pitched vocal chops and a beatbox kit (lip "b" kick, "pf" snare, "ts" hats). One sound per event in each world: sand hitting metal at every mode change, laptop keys per letter, a CRT hum, a tuned drop per greeting, wind for the flight, a gas ignition and crackles, a needle drop with vinyl crackle (the music goes through an old speaker), ice tinkles for the frost. Narration checked with Whisper. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: a voice analyser that writes the loudness, the spectrogram and the waveform, one Remotion composition with nine blocks (four 2D canvases and five fragment shaders) and the music, SFX and narration on the timeline.

05 · What Claude built

A different material in every block.

SAND

Grains on the nodal lines

Each grain starts at a random spot and takes Newton steps toward the zero line of the plate's mode. The voice's loudness adds the bounce, so the sand jumps on every word.

SCOPE

The picture is the sound

The figure is drawn from x and y signals, and the same two signals play as the left and right audio channels in that block.

WATER

Standing waves, one shader

Waves from N directions add up into a symmetric pattern; the surface normal reflects a ring light, which turns the ridges into bright rings.

CANYON

A sentence you can fly over

The spectrogram image is the height map: words become mountains, pauses become plains. A raymarcher finds the ground and fog hides the edge.

key code · TypeScript + GLSL
// sand: every grain slides onto the nodal lines
const st = F / (gx * gx + gy * gy); x -= st * gx; y -= st * gy;

// water: standing waves from N directions
for (k = 0; k < N; k++) h += cos(K * dot(q, dir(PI * k / N)) + phase);

// canyon: the height is the voice spectrogram
float h = pow(texture(uSpec, vec2(z / 270.0, abs(x) / 34.0)).r, 1.6) * 15.0;
LEVEL UP

Music made of voices

Every instrument is a synthesized voice: a glottal pulse through three formant filters is a choir, a nasal "mm" is the bass, and the drums are a beatbox kit. In the vinyl block the whole track goes through an old speaker.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Backwards smoothstep

smoothstep(a + w, a - w, p) goes from 1 to 0. For something that grows with progress, the low edge goes first.

Zero-length strokes

Particles drawn as a line from their last position vanish once they stop moving. Switch to small squares when they land.

Map text to its real width

Sample the text's pixels and use their actual right edge, or a short sentence fills only half of the waveform.

The sun in the fog

A bright sun plus fog flattens a landscape to haze. Push the sun aside, darken the low rock and thin the fog.

Empty first frames

Flames that ignite, frost that grows and text that types all start from nothing. Start each one already a little in, so every block opens on something.

Read the voice back

Whisper heard every narration line correctly; the TTS reads "ElevenLabs" as "eleven labs", which is right.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Sand grains, phosphor lines and frost needles smear when compressed. Encode at 14 Mbps CBR.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the most striking settled frame (here, the water once its pattern has formed). The profile grid crops covers to 3:4, so keep the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the credits.

"Spec ad · not affiliated with [brand]", which voice narrates it, and the references you learned from.

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
