01 · The idea

One line, the whole app.

Duolingo already sells learning as a path: lesson after lesson, one node at a time. So the spot is one glowing green line that starts under the wordmark and travels through every part of the product. Flat, bright and quick, like the app itself, on a dark green stage that makes each feature's color glow.

It is a spec piece. Duo and the wordmark are Duolingo's official assets; the film was designed and built by the Lab.

Scenes, in order:
1. 0-3.5s: Duo slams in with a squash and stretch. The wordmark appears, and the green path starts under it and runs down through lesson nodes that light up one by one, ending on a START button that gets pressed. Headline "5 minutes a day."
2. 3.5-6.4s: START shrinks into a phone-sized lesson card: "Translate this sentence", Duo with the bubble "Quiero un café, por favor.", word tiles snap into "I'd like a coffee, please.", CHECK is pressed and the green "Excellent!" banner slides up. Headline "One word at a time."
3. 6.4-9.2s: A streak flame grows, the counter rolls up toward 365 with "day streak" under it, and the week dots fill from M to S. Headline "A streak you'll keep."
4. 9.2-12s: A Diamond League table. "You" climbs from #7 toward the top while the XP counts up, with a green highlight on the row. Headline "A league you'll win."
5. 12-14.7s: A video call card: Duo says "¡Hola! ¿Qué tal tu día?", a voice waveform plays, and the reply "¡Muy bien! Pedí un café en español." Headline "Then... you just talk."
6. 14.7-18s: The path branches into four course nodes that pop in with their icons: Languages (with small flags), Math, Music and Chess, each word lighting up in its color.
7. 18-24s: Everything folds back into the line. Duo lands on it, the wordmark resolves under him, the line "One path, every skill." appears, then the wordmark alone.

Audio. Compose an original track in code: 100 BPM in D major, marimba, felt keys and a soft sub, a gentle kick and clap, with the drop when the league starts. 05 · What Claude built

A spot that is actually a web page.

STRUCTURE

HyperFrames scenes

A root index.html places seven scenes on the timeline. Each scene is its own HTML file with a paused GSAP timeline that HyperFrames steps through frame by frame, so every render comes out identical.

MASCOT

The real Duo, in parts

A small script splits the official SVG into groups for the wings, eyes and beak, so Duo can flap, blink and talk without ever being redrawn.

MUSIC

Composed in code

A warm 100 BPM track in D major with marimba, felt keys and a soft sub, written for this spot only. The drop lands on the league scene.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS

Eight short lines, one per beat, placed on each headline. The music ducks under the voice and the mix sits at −14 LUFS.

// the flame counts 1 → 365

  duration: 1.1,
  ease: "power2.out"});
// your row climbs #7 → #1

  duration: 0.75,
  ease: "power3.inOut"});

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

One line connects everything

The path is the brand's own idea. Every scene grows out of the same green line, so the spot never feels like a list of features.

02

A dark stage, the app's colors as light

On dark green, the feature colors glow: orange for the streak, blue for the league, purple for music. Each color appears once, on its own word.

03

The real Duo, animated by parts

The official mascot carries the brand. The personality comes from small moves (a flap, a blink, a beak that talks), not from redrawing him.

04

UI rebuilt, not recorded

Every card is rebuilt in HTML from the app's real patterns, so it stays sharp at 1080×1920 and moves exactly on the beat.

05

Fast, but readable

About three seconds per beat. When the first cut felt rushed, the fix was to stretch the whole timeline, not to cut content.

06

Stills before render

Every scene is checked against the safe zone as a still. Fixing a frame takes seconds; fixing a render takes minutes.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

01

The first cut read too fast

The first version was 21 seconds and felt rushed. The fix was stretching the whole timeline to 24 (about three seconds per beat), not cutting features.

02

Animate the real Duo by parts

Split the official SVG into groups with ids (wings, eyes, beak) before animating, so Duo can flap, blink and talk without being redrawn.

03

Cut on the beat, not with fades

Crossfades leave two half scenes on screen. Hard cuts on the music's beat read cleaner on a phone.

04

Readable on a phone

Headlines at 44 px or more, no text on screen for less than 1.5 seconds, and check the stills scaled down to 390 px wide.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Renderers often come out at 5–6 Mbps on flat designs, and Instagram compresses again. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the product best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the headline and the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
