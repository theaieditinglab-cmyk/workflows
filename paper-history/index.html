01 · The idea

Fifteen years, one sheet at a time.

A history told as a stack of paper. Every year gets one sheet: a typed label on top, a year card, and one object that shows how motion was made then (a graph editor, identical templates, a pile of requests, an image without motion). The sheet lands, says its one thing, and leaves to the left. The last sheet is today: a prompt and a polaroid with a real ad playing inside it.

It is the Lab's own piece, so there is no brand to borrow from. It uses only the Lab's colors and fonts, and it says nothing out loud: the labels and the music carry it.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

The look comes from a paper style the Lab had already built for client stories (our recipe EM02). This piece keeps its sheets, tape labels and stamps, and drops its stop-motion jitter.

Style

Paper-cut history: torn sheets that drop in, typed tape labels, pencil that draws itself, ink stamps. Smooth motion, no stop-motion shake.

Story

Five dated steps, 2012 → 2015 → 2020 → 2023 → 2027, from "every keyframe by hand" to "Claude writes the motion. In code.", then one closing line.

04 · Claude session

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 32-second vertical paper-cut film called "Motion History" in Remotion (React), 1080x1920 at 30 fps, 960 frames. Text and music only, no voice, all in English. The idea: "Motion design took 15 years to get here. Now it's a conversation." Every value must be a function of the current frame, so renders are identical.

Look. Use ONLY these colors: cream paper #F6F0E6 for the background, white #FFFFFF for the sheets, ink #141210, clay #D97757 for pencil and accents, clay deep #B9573A for stamps and the vignette, tape grey #E9E3DA and a muted #5E5850 for small captions. Fonts: Bricolage Grotesque 800 for years and big lines, Geist for body text, Geist Mono 500 for typed labels.
- Background: cream paper with a fiber texture (SVG feTurbulence, baseFrequency "0.012 0.6", 35% opacity, multiply), a warm clay-deep vignette at the edges (about 18%) and a light grain (12%).
- NO stop-motion jitter. Every move is smooth and eased. Nothing shakes.
- Sheets: white paper with torn edges (clip-path polygon with random teeth from a stable seed). Sheet box x 120, y 600, 840x820, rotated -2 or +2 degrees, alternating. Each sheet drops in from 180 px above, rotated +8 degrees at 112% scale, and lands in 0.3 s while its shadow shrinks. It ALWAYS exits to the left: 1300 px in about 0.42 s, rotating -6 degrees. Same direction for the whole film so the eye knows where to look.
- Labels: one tape strip per sheet at y 290, centered, rotated -1.5 degrees, Geist Mono 40 px in ink, typed at 22 characters per second with a blinking "_". The label fades out just before its sheet leaves.
- Years: an ink card with torn edges, the year in cream Bricolage Grotesque 800 at 132 px with a period ("2012."), springing in over the top corner of the sheet, left and right alternating.
- Pencil: clay strokes that draw themselves (stroke-dashoffset with pathLength 1).
- Stamps: clay-deep border and text in Bricolage Grotesque 800, scaling from 180% to 100% in 5 frames, multiply blend.

Scenes (120 BPM, one beat = 15 frames, every entrance on a beat):
0. 0-2.6 s: frame 0 is already moving. A torn ink card "MOTION / DESIGN" springs in, a keyframe curve draws itself in clay pencil under it with three ink keyframe diamonds popping in, plus a baseline. At 2.2 s the whole opener slides out to the left.
1. 2.5-6.3 s, 2012: "After Effects. Every keyframe by hand." Graph editor on the sheet: a grid, the caption "graph editor · position", an ink bezier curve that draws itself, three clay keyframe diamonds and two pencil tangent handles.
2. 6.5-10.3 s, 2015: "Templates and plugins. Everything looks the same." Six identical template cards drop in one by one (3 frames apart), then the stamp "TEMPLATE #4,219".
3. 10.5-14.3 s, 2020: "Every brand wants motion. Teams stretched thin." Five request notes pile up with a spring: "Need a reel by Friday", "Can we animate this?", "Make it pop ✦", "One more version…", and a clay one, "Launch is tomorrow".
4. 14.5-18.3 s, 2023: "AI makes images. Motion is still by hand." A clay gradient card "image.png" with "Images ✓", an ink card with clay bars with "Motion ✗", and a pencil line under it.
5. 18.5-23.8 s, 2027: "Claude writes the motion. In code." A prompt card with the Claude spark: "[one line of a real prompt you used]". A polaroid with "rendered in code" scales in and plays [my real ad, muted] inside it. At 20.5 s the stamp "DONE." hits.
6. 24-28.2 s: on the plain paper, the label "15 years to get here." types itself; at 25.7 s "Now it's a" (Geist 52) and "CONVERSATION." (Bricolage 800, 108 px) spring in, then a spinning clay spark. Fade out at 27.7 s.
7. 28-32 s: end card with [my logo] and "Want the full workflow? Comment [KEYWORD]".

Keep every label, year card, sheet and the end card inside the Reels safe zone: x 90-960 and y 250-1480.

Audio. Compose an original track in code (numpy + scipy), 120 BPM, warm and handmade: a jazzy FM electric piano (Rhodes) in E-flat, an upright bass, brushes and vinyl crackle. One chord per bar: Ebmaj9, Cm9, Abmaj7, Bb13. It grows year by year: vinyl and one Rhodes chord at the opener, Rhodes comping from 2012, bass from 2015, half-time drums (soft kick, long brush on 3, short brushes on eighths) from 2020, a Rhodes melody from 2023, a soft impact and the full band at 2027, a short breath at "15 years", the groove back on "CONVERSATION" and a long Eb chord ringing out to the end. Light reverb, soft clipping, 1.5 s fade. SFX few and soft, in a stationery world, each on a real event: paper drop when a sheet lands, cardboard when a year card lands, a typewriter tick every two characters, a paper slide panned left when a sheet leaves, pencil scratches on the strokes, card flicks on keyframes and templates, sticky notes on the requests, a stamp thump, a camera shutter on the polaroid, a thunk on CONVERSATION and one single chime at the end. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process. First make a 6-second test (opener + 2012) at half resolution. Before the full render, export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked so I can review them. Wait for my approval, apply my notes, and only then render the final MP4, plus a second version without sound.

05 · What Claude built

Five components, one stack of paper.

STRUCTURE

Scenes as data

Each year is one line: start frame, end frame, year, label and a function that draws its object. The same Sheet, Label and Year components render all five, so every sheet behaves the same.

PAPER

Torn edges in code

A clip-path polygon with small random teeth on all four sides. The randomness comes from Remotion's seeded random(), so each sheet tears the same way in every render.

TYPE

A typed label

The label shows frames × 22 / 30 characters and a "_" that blinks every 8 frames, in Geist Mono on a torn tape strip.

MUSIC

Composed in code

A jazzy FM Rhodes in E-flat, upright bass, half-time brushes and vinyl, adding one layer per year. SFX are placed on the same frames as the sheets, labels and stamps.

key code · tsx (MotionHistory.tsx)
// a sheet that drops in (0.3 s) and always leaves to the left (0.42 s)
const Sheet = ({f, at, out, seed, x, y, w, h, rot = -2, children}) => {
  if (f < at || f > out + 14) return null;
  const land = interpolate(f, [at, at + 9], [0, 1], {...clamp, easing: Easing.bezier(0.3, 0.7, 0.2, 1)});
  const exit = interpolate(f, [out, out + 13], [0, 1], {...clamp, easing: Easing.bezier(0.6, 0, 0.8, 0.4)});
  return (
    <div style={{position: 'absolute', left: x - exit * 1300, top: y - (1 - land) * 180, width: w, height: h,
      transform: `rotate(${rot + (1 - land) * 8 - exit * 6}deg) scale(${1 + (1 - land) * 0.12})`}}>
      <div style={{position: 'absolute', inset: 0, background: LAB.paper, clipPath: torn(seed)}}>{children}</div>
    </div>
  );
};

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

Smooth, not shaky

The paper style it comes from used stop-motion jitter. Here it was rejected, so every drop and exit is an eased curve and nothing trembles.

02

Always exit left

Every sheet, the opener and every year card leave the same way. The eye learns the rule on the first sheet and goes straight to the next one.

03

Brand colors only

Cream, ink and clay, plus white paper and grey tape. Fifteen years of history and not one color from outside the Lab's palette.

04

One object per year

A graph editor, six identical cards, a pile of notes, a check and a cross. Each sheet shows its idea as a thing, not as a sentence.

05

Real proof at the end

The 2027 sheet doesn't describe what Claude makes; it plays a real ad made in code inside a polaroid, then stamps it "DONE."

06

Music that grows

One layer per year (Rhodes, bass, drums, melody, full band), so the sound tells the same story as the paper: more and more is possible.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Jitter reads as a mistake

Stop-motion shake on the paper was rejected. Keep the paper style, drop the 12-steps-per-second wobble, and ease every move.

Colors that drift

The Lab's colors must not change from video to video. Put them in one theme file and import it, instead of picking colors per scene.

Mixed exit directions

If one sheet leaves right and the next leaves left, the viewer looks the wrong way. Every sheet exits the same way, to the left.

A recycled sound

Each video gets its own music and its own SFX. This one is a jazzy Rhodes in E-flat with brushes and vinyl; the Lab's other pieces use different keys, instruments and sounds.

A low bitrate

On flat pieces like this one, Remotion's bitrate flag still comes out around 5–6 Mbps. Re-encode with x264 at a constant 14 Mbps before posting.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Renderers often come out at 5–6 Mbps on flat designs, and Instagram compresses again. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the product best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the headline and the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the credits.

Say in one line how it was made and credit anything on screen that isn't yours. If you remake a real brand, end with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
cover + caption · claude code
Export the strongest frame of the final video as a 1080x1920 PNG for the Instagram cover. Show me how it looks cropped to 3:4 (the middle 1080x1440) and make sure the headline and the product sit inside that crop. Then write a caption under 150 words: one hook line, two lines on how it was made, and end with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

Before you post: everything inside x 90–960 and y 250–1480 · no text on screen for less than 1.5 s · something moves on frame 0 · −14 LUFS · 12–16 Mbps · the disclaimer in the caption.
