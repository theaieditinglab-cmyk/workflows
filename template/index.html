01 · The idea

The logo is a block.

In Notion everything is a block, and the Notion logo is literally a block. So the logo is not stamped on at the end: it is the main character. It builds the ad, and the ad keeps building it out of cubes, a different way each time, so the brand is on screen from the first frame to the last.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab with the official logo, the brand's colors and type close to Notion's own.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

A white world is elegant, but only if every scene has a clear shape, real shadows and something to build.

The official logo

The SVG path of the Notion logo. Claude rasterizes it into cubes, extrudes it into a smooth 3D logo and paints it on the faces of a real 3D block.

One unit, four builds

The logo prints itself, is poured like sand, becomes a mosaic of templates and rises out of the field. Same unit, never the same move twice.

Palette

Paper white, ink black and Notion's pastel tag colors, nothing else.

#F7F6F3#191919#D3E5EF#FBF3DB#F5E0E9#DBEDDB
Type

Inter Tight 800 for the first line, Instrument Serif italic for the second, a serif 3D font for "Write" and JetBrains Mono for the labels.

Pace

One scene and one headline about every 3 seconds, something moving on frame 0, and every time the logo is built it gets about 1.5 seconds to form and half a second to be read.

Format

9:16, 24 s, 30 fps, with every headline, menu, label and the logo inside the Reels safe zone.

Sound

A vibraphone café track made in code, wood, sand and paper sounds for every block event and a seven-line narration.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: a 3-second test, stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 24-second vertical spec ad for Notion called "Building Blocks" in Remotion (React) with @remotion/three, 1080x1920 at 30 fps. The idea: in Notion everything is a block, and the Notion logo is itself a block, so the logo is the main character. It builds the ad, and the ad keeps building the logo out of cubes, a different way each time. Render with --gl=angle. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

Look: a paper-white world (#F7F6F3) with soft clay-like 3D blocks, a studio light with soft shadows (PCF soft shadow maps, a hemisphere fill and a drei Environment with two Lightformers), black ink (#191919) for anything that should read, and Notion's pastel tag colors as the only color. Headlines in Inter Tight 800 with a second line in Instrument Serif italic.

The logo: use the official Notion SVG path. Rasterize it on a canvas with Path2D (white outer shape, black logo on top) at 44x44, and turn every filled pixel into one cube of a single InstancedMesh, black or white by its pixel. Also extrude the same path with SVGLoader + ExtrudeGeometry for the smooth 3D logo (pure black, roughness 0.9, low env reflections, or the ink renders grey). Build the 3D "Notion block" as a real box with the logo faces drawn on canvas textures: the front with the N, a white top with a black border, black sides.

Scenes, in order:
1. 0-3s "It starts with a block.": the logo prints itself out of the field. The cubes rise from under the floor row by row, bottom to top, the first rows already moving on frame 0, so the whole logo is complete at about 1.4 s. Hold it still for half a second so it reads. Then it collapses: every cube falls with gravity onto one cell of a 14x26 cube field, the cell bounces, and the cells rise into the layout of a page (draw the layout into a 14x26 canvas and use each pixel as a target height). A block menu ("/block": Text, To-do list, Board, Calendar, Image) appears once the logo has fallen.
2. 3-6s "Write anything.": the letters "Write" in 3D (TextGeometry, gentilis bold) fall with real physics (120 steps per second, a bounce that loses 62% of its speed, rotation that settles), and the period is the Notion block: 1,000 small cubes pour into its place layer by layer like sand, and when it is full it becomes the smooth Notion block.
3. 6-9s "Plan it your way.": 35 blocks with real card content hop from a board to a calendar to a table.
4. 9-12s "Think out loud.": a line drawing of a person at a laptop draws itself stroke by stroke, and the Notion logo appears on the laptop screen.
5. 12-16s "Every piece, one page.": the Notion block drops in and opens like a box (each face turns on its bottom edge, the lid hangs from the back face; outside is the logo, inside is white paper), and the paper becomes a page that separates into floating layers.
6. 16-20s "Start from a template.": about 900 template cards, one per cell of the logo at 30x30 (dark cards for the logo's ink, white cards for its face), flip in from the center while the camera pulls back about nine times until the wall reads as the Notion logo. Put the headline on a paper card so it reads over dark cards.
7. 20-24s: about 1,600 cubes rise out of the field into the logo facing the camera and fuse into the smooth 3D logo. Then "Notion" and "Build anything, block by block." End there.

Keep every headline, menu, label and the logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. One idea per 3-second block, no text on screen for less than 1.5 seconds.

Audio: an original studio-cafe track made in code (numpy) in G major at 100 BPM: vibraphone chords with tremolo, a walking upright bass, finger snaps and a wood block. SFX on every real event: wooden ticks that climb in pitch as the logo prints, a click when it locks, a rain of cubes and a low thump when it collapses, a keyboard thock for "/", grains of sand for the period, paper hinges as the block opens, flicks as the templates flip, air as the camera pulls back and a chord when the mosaic reads, a cascade as the cubes rise and a click when they fuse. Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice am_michael: "It starts with a block." / "Write anything." / "Plan it your way." / "Think out loud." / "Every piece fits on one page." / "Or start from a template." / "Notion. Build anything." Check every line with Whisper. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: first render a 3-second test of the opening at half resolution and check it at 10 frames per second. Then export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked, and mark where the logo appears. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: one Remotion composition with one three.js world: the logo in cubes, the cube field, the 3D letters with the sand period, the layout blocks, the box that opens, the wall of templates and the smooth logo, a camera per scene, the block menu, the drawing and the headlines on top, and the music, SFX and narration on the timeline.

05 · What Claude built

An ad made of its own logo.

LOGO

The SVG as cubes

The official path is drawn on a 44 by 44 canvas with Path2D. Each filled pixel becomes one cube of a single instanced mesh, black or white, so 1,700 cubes cost one draw call.

BOX

A block that opens

Six faces on hinges: the sides turn on their bottom edges and the lid hangs from the back. Outside is the logo, inside is paper, and the paper becomes the page.

MOSAIC

900 templates, one logo

The same raster at 30 by 30 decides which template cards are dark and which are white. Close up they are templates; nine times farther away they are the logo.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS

Seven short lines in the Michael voice, picked from three samples. The music ducks under the voice and the mix sits at −14 LUFS with a true peak at −1.4 dB.

key code · three.js
// the official logo, one cube per pixel (no image to load: Path2D draws it instantly)
const g = canvas(44, 44).getContext('2d');
g.scale(44 / 24, 44 / 24);
g.fillStyle = '#fff'; g.fill(new Path2D(NOTION_OUTER));
g.fillStyle = '#000'; g.fill(new Path2D(NOTION_D));
const cubes = pixels(g).filter(p => p.alpha > 0);        // about 1,700 cubes

// it prints itself: each row rises a little after the one below
const start = -0.3 + row(n) * 1.05;                       // 0 = bottom row
const u = ease(clamp((t - start) / 0.38));
o.position.set(slot.x, lerp(-0.4, slot.y, u), slot.z);
o.updateMatrix(); mesh.setMatrixAt(n, o.matrix);

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

The logo inside the story

A logo pasted on a corner or a card does nothing. Here it builds the page, becomes a period, opens into a page and hides in a wall of templates.

02

Four builds, never the same

Printing, sand, a mosaic and cubes rising: the same idea each time, a different move each time.

03

Slow enough to read

An explosion that lasts less than a second looks busy and says nothing. A build of 1.5 seconds and a half-second hold reads on a phone.

04

Clay white, ink black

A paper-white world with soft shadows matches the brand. Anything that has to read is ink black.

05

Open and close with the logo

The ad starts with the logo being built and ends with the logo being built, so the spot feels like one piece.

06

Stills before render

Rounds of stills, plus the fast scenes at 10 frames per second, caught every problem in minutes.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Too fast to read

If the logo explodes or forms in under a second, nobody sees it. Give each build about 1.5 seconds and hold it still for half a second.

Check fast scenes densely

A strip every 1.5 seconds hides what happens in between. Pull fast moments again at 10 frames per second before you approve them.

Black that renders grey

A glossy black logo reflects the studio light and turns grey. Use pure black, roughness 0.9 and low environment reflections.

Headlines over dark tiles

Black text disappears on a mosaic of dark cards. Put the headline on a paper card.

3D words in a vertical frame

A word that fits in a square test gets cut off in 9:16. Make the letters smaller and pull the camera back until the whole word sits inside the safe zone.

Empty first frames

Start every scene with something already on screen: the first rows of the logo are already rising on frame 0.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Soft shadows on white band when compressed. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame where the logo is being built, with the brand name on top. The feed crops covers to 4:5 and the profile grid to 3:4, so keep everything between y 300 and 1480.

Step 04

A short caption.

What the brand's style is, that it was made with Claude Code, the keyword, and end with "Inspired by @brand".

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 28M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "alimiter=limit=0.8:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
