01 · The idea

Many sources, one answer.

AI answer engines usually get ads with a chat box and glowing text. Perplexity's difference is visible in every answer: the sources come first, and every line carries a numbered citation. So that became the rhythm of the ad. Each block asks, gathers, answers and cites, and every citation lands on a beat with its own sound.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. Perplexity's brand page gives official logo files, so the lockup is used as is, flat and untouched, and nothing else in the ad imitates the logo.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

AI product launches are what gets watched and commissioned the most when made with Claude. The references were Perplexity's own art direction (open skies, books flying up, streams that converge, landscapes cut into blocks, depth of field) and editorial motion reels with big serif type.

One rhythm, many techniques

A physical sky in JavaScript, bending pages in three.js, a 2D canvas of streams with sub-frame motion blur, an exponential zoom, a rack focus with per-card blur, CSS 3D slabs and a variable serif. All of them answer a question and cite it.

Brand rules

Use the official files from perplexity.ai/hub/brand and never modify them. The brand avoids glow, blue-purple haze, glass and robots, so the ad does too. The caption carries the spec-ad disclaimer.

05 · What Claude built

An ad that answers like the product.

SKY

Physics, not a gradient

For each pixel, a ray marches through the atmosphere and adds up the light scattered by air (Rayleigh) and haze (Mie). Done in JavaScript at 180×320 and scaled up: it looks real and costs the GPU nothing.

FOCUS

Depth of field by hand

Each card is projected with a simple perspective and blurred by its distance to the focus plane. Keep the blur at 12 px or less and the chosen card in front, or it turns into fog.

MUSIC

Composed in code

A library at sunset in E major at 120 BPM: harp arpeggios, a breathing string pad, soft kick, rim and a low tom, with a breather on the focus scene.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS, checked by Whisper

Eight short lines in the Heart voice, all heard correctly on the first pass. Mix at −14 LUFS, true peak −2.1 dB.

key code · TypeScript
// the sky: single scattering, Rayleigh + Mie, per pixel (Nishita)
const mu = dot(dir, sun);
const pR = 3 / (16 * PI) * (1 + mu * mu);
const pM = 3 / (8 * PI) * ((1 - G * G) * (1 + mu * mu)) / ((2 + G * G) * (1 + G * G - 2 * G * mu) ** 1.5);
sky[c] = 20 * (sumR[c] * BR[c] * pR + sumM[c] * BM * pM);

// rack focus: blur grows with the distance to the focus plane
const blur = Math.min(12, Math.abs(card.z - focusZ) * 0.009);
style.filter = `blur(${blur}px)`;

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

The signature as the rhythm

Sources in, one answer out, a citation on every line. Viewers feel the product before they read its name.

02

Away from the chat box

No glowing chat bubbles or metal blobs. An open sky, paper and focus say "knowledge" the way the brand's own art direction does.

03

Real where code is real

Sky, light and depth of field look filmed in code; modeled objects don't. So the ad is built from light, paper and type.

04

The logo untouched

The official lockup, Off Black on Off White, low and centered, the brand's first choice of placement.

05

Facts still matter

A science ad can't get the science wrong. The answer on screen was fixed to "Shorter wavelengths, like blue, scatter the most."

06

Stills before render

Three rounds of stills caught the flat sky, the buried text, the messy focus and every empty first frame in minutes.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Stair-step horizons

A low-resolution sky with a hard land edge comes out in steps once scaled up. Blend the ridge with fractional coverage over one pixel.

Blocks that start empty

If the headline and objects enter after the cut, the first frame of the block is empty. Start them a few frames before the cut so they are already arriving.

Streams over the answer

Draw the moving cards under the answer card, not over it, or the answer can't be read while it writes in.

Fonts that vanish

A web font request can fail mid-render and silently fall back. Load local files and wait for them before rendering.

Clips that cross a cut

In the how-it-was-made video, each clip has to start at the beginning of its scene, or it runs into the next scene halfway through.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Smooth skies and soft blur band when compressed. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the most striking frame. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
