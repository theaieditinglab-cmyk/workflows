01 · The idea

One key does everything.

Cursor is a code editor that writes what comes next. Developers describe using it in one word: Tab. It accepts what Cursor predicted, and the next Tab takes you to the next place you were going to edit. So the whole ad is built around that key.

The first version put a Tab button on top of every scene, and it looked pasted on. The fix was to make the key part of the world: the ad happens inside a keyboard, the Tab key is a real key in it, and everything that happens physically comes out of it, as a ring of light running between the keys.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: a short test, stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 24-second vertical spec ad for Cursor called "Just hit Tab." in Remotion (React + @remotion/three), 1080x1920 at 30 fps. The idea: Cursor's whole magic is one key, Tab. It finishes your sentence and knows where you're going next. So the entire ad happens inside a backlit 3D keyboard, and every single thing that happens is triggered by the real Tab key in the scene. Never put a Tab button on top of the image: the key lives in the world and the action comes out of it. Make every value a function of the current frame.

The world: a QWERTY keyboard built with instanced RoundedBoxGeometry keycaps (real layout, 1 unit per key), legends from a canvas atlas, dark matte caps (#2E2B26) lit by an environment of Lightformers. Under the keys sits a floor of warm amber light (#F5A524), so light only shows through the gaps between the keys. Every Tab press sends a ring of light out from the Tab key through those gaps. Around the keyboard there's a plain of blank keys hidden under the floor.

Scenes, on a 100 BPM grid (one beat = 18 frames):
1. 0-3s: the flat keyboard seen diagonally. "I was thinking we could" is typed: each key sinks and glows as its letter is typed. Cursor suggests "get tacos?" in gray. The camera drops to a macro of the tab key. Tab: the sentence is accepted. Headline: "You know that someone who finishes your sentences?"
2. 3-6s: Tab again. The ring of light runs through the gaps and every key it touches rises into a skyscraper with lit code windows, keeping its letter on the roof. The plain of keys comes up out of the floor as the wave reaches it. "Developers have one too."
3. 6-9s: a floating code panel (cart.ts) above a tall building, the next line already waiting in gray. Tab: the wave runs down the street, climbs the building as a line of light and accepts the line. "It writes what's next."
4. 9-12s: Tab. Tab. Tab. on the beat. Each press launches the golden cursor (the arrow from the Cursor logo) to another file, where it stops against a bar like the ⇥ symbol and the waiting change is written. The camera pulls back a little on every jump so you see where it leaves and where it lands. Big words "Tab." stack up at the bottom with each press.
5. 12-15s: Tab: arcs of light connect the files across the city. "It knows the whole codebase."
6. 15-18s: four Tabs on the beat, each one lights a quarter of the city while the golden cursor flies over it. "You move. It keeps up."
7. 18-21s: Tab: the camera rises to a top view. The lit rooftops draw the Cursor cube (rasterize the official logo onto the key grid: three faces in three tones, the arrow in white keys) with a thin hexagon outline. "It doesn't wait for orders."
8. 21-24s: the last Tab is the key itself seen from above. Its ring wraps the logo of keys, which becomes the official logo, the CURSOR wordmark and "The AI code editor."

Keep every headline, panel and logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480, and something already moving on frame 0.

Audio: an original track made in code (numpy): warm electronic, 100 BPM in D minor, a plucked arpeggio that sounds like typing, a pad and a sub that enter with the city, and a hole in the arpeggio on every Tab so the key always sounds dry and big. One sound per event: key clicks while typing, a deep Tab thunk with a little sparkle, the rumble of keys rising, glass for the panels, a whoosh for each jump and a click as it lands on the bar, tuned pings for the arcs, an impact and a big chord on the logo. Narration with Kokoro TTS (voice af_bella at 1.15 speed): "You know that someone who finishes your sentences?" / "Developers have one too." / "It writes what comes next," / "Tab." "Tab." "Tab." / "It knows your whole codebase." / "You move. It keeps up." / "It doesn't wait for orders." / "Cursor. Just hit Tab." Check every line with Whisper. Master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: build a 3-second test of the key moment first (the Tab key sinking, the light running through the gaps, the keys rising), then a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds with the safe zone marked. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: a 3-second test first. The keyboard was almost black, the camera got stuck between the first keys that rose, and the light wave was hard to see. The fixes: light from above plus a warm floor that shows through the gaps, low keys in front and the tall ones behind like a skyline, and the camera rising right after the press.

Tell me if the keyboard reads as a keyboard on frame 0, if the Tab key always feels part of the world, if the camera ever gets stuck between rising keys, if anything leaves the safe zone, and if a viewer who has never used Cursor would get the story from the images alone.

What came back: the sentence got lost behind a key, the brand was missing in the first seconds, the cursor jumped off the edge and the logo of keys didn't read. 05 · What Claude built

A keyboard that becomes a city.

LIGHT

A floor that only shows in the gaps

The light lives under the keys. The caps hide it, so every Tab reads as a ring running through the gaps of the keyboard, like a real backlight.

CITY

Keys that rise when the ring reaches them

Each key knows its distance to the Tab key, so the city grows outward from it, and every building keeps its letter on the roof.

LOGO

Drawn by rooftops

The official logo is rasterized onto the key grid. From above, three tones of lit rooftops make the cube and white keys make the arrow, before the real logo takes over.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS, checked by Whisper

Bella at 1.15 speed, with "Tab." spoken on the beat of each jump. Mix at −14 LUFS, true peak −1.6 dB.

07 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

A button pasted on top

A key drawn over every scene reads as an add-on. If the gesture matters, it has to live in the 3D world and cause what happens.

A camera inside the buildings

Keys that rise next to a low camera become walls. Keep the front keys low and lift the camera right after the press.

Emissive that burns

Making a whole keycap glow turns the city into flat white blocks. Light the windows and the rooftops, never the whole cap.

A logo that doesn't read

A logo made of a coarse grid needs clear tones per face and an outline, and should hand over to the official logo quickly.

08 · Ship it

From render to post.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Render at 14 Mbps so the dark city and the thin light don't turn into blocks after Instagram compresses it.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. If the peak is a little high, a limiter on the audio fixes it without touching the video.

Step 03

Make the cover.

The profile grid crops covers to 3:4, so keep the logo and the line "Just hit Tab." between y 300 and 1480.

Step 04

Caption.

Short and clear, and end with "Inspired by @[brand]".
