01 · The idea

One gift, opened slowly.

A Ferrero Rocher is a gift before it is a chocolate: the gold foil, the paper cup, the hazelnut you find inside. So the spot unwraps one slowly, shows what is inside, and turns that one gift into a whole Christmas.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab with the official logo, the brand’s gold and a chocolate built entirely in code.

02 · References

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) for your system.

Step 02

Install Node 22+ and FFmpeg.

Start a Remotion project with npx create-video@latest, add @remotion/three, three, @react-three/fiber and @react-three/drei, and open Claude Code inside that folder.

Step 03

A free studio light.

Download a small studio HDRI from Poly Haven (CC0) into public/. It is what makes the foil and the chocolate shine.

Step 04

Python and a GPU-friendly render.

numpy and scipy compose the music and SFX, and Kokoro TTS reads the narration. Pass --gl=angle to every render and still command, or the canvas comes out black.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: a half-resolution test, stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 20-second vertical Christmas spec ad for Ferrero Rocher called "The Golden Gift" in Remotion (React) with @remotion/three, 1080x1920 at 30 fps, all in code: the chocolate, the gold foil, the snow, the tree, the toys and the music. No photos and no image AI. Render with --gl=angle. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

The story, in one continuous snowy night: a single Ferrero Rocher sits in the snow in its brown paper cup, with a red ribbon and a small "For you" tag. Its gold foil opens like a flower, the chocolate rises out of it and splits in half to show what is inside. Then the chocolates fall one by one and build a Christmas tree, a toy train circles it, Santa flies in on his sleigh, lands in the snow in front of the tree and leaves the gifts. The star lights up: the golden gift.

The chocolate: a sphere covered in hazelnut bits (scatter 90 small rough pieces over it) with a dark chocolate material. The foil: 7 petals built as their own meshes with a crumpled, metallic gold material, hinged at the bottom so they can open. The official logo goes on the white label on top. Use a small studio HDRI so the foil and the chocolate shine.

The cut: when it splits, show a believable inside, not a perfect diagram. Paint the cut face pixel by pixel on a canvas: the chocolate shell with nut bits, the crisp wafer, the cream and a whole hazelnut in the middle (a slightly irregular ellipse with its tip and a small cavity). Use value noise so the edges are never perfectly round, and read the same texture mirrored on both halves.

The tree: 65 chocolates fall in order and land on 9 rings that get smaller toward the top, each with its own little bounce. Gifts sit under the tree. A wooden toy train runs on a circular track around it.

Santa: a lacquered toy Santa driving a sleigh pulled by two toy reindeer. Move the sleigh along a Catmull-Rom spline: it comes in from the side, flies around and over the tree, comes back and lands with an ease-out in the snow in front of the tree, outside the track. It drops 5 gift boxes in the snow as it lands. Rotate it with Euler angles (bank, yaw, pitch), never with setFromUnitVectors, so it does not flip.

Scenes:
1. 0-4s "A little gift, wrapped in gold.": the closed chocolate in the snow, the label jumps, the foil opens petal by petal.
2. 4-8s "Made with care, layer by layer.": the chocolate rises and splits in half, the camera moves in slowly.
3. 8-10.4s "With a whole hazelnut at its heart.": the cut face fills the frame.
4. 10.5-15.5s "Because the best gifts are shared.": a gold flash, the chocolates build the tree, the train starts.
5. 12.6-19s: Santa's sleigh flies in, circles the tree, comes back and lands in front with the gifts.
6. 16-20s "The golden gift.": the star lights up, the lights turn on in a spiral and the official logo appears above the tree.

Keep every headline and the logo inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480. Show the logo at the start (the label), in the middle (a small signature and the labels on the tree) and at the end. One idea per block of about 3 seconds.

Audio: an original Christmas waltz made in code (numpy) in 3/4 at 120 BPM in D major: music box, pizzicato, soft strings and very quiet sleigh bells, intimate during the cut and full when the star lights up. Sounds tied to events: the paper tick of the label, one metallic crinkle per petal, the crack of the wafer with crumbs, one tuned "tink" per chocolate that lands on the tree, a toy train chuff with wooden wheel knocks, sleigh bells that get louder as the sleigh comes close, a soft puff when it lands in the snow, a thump for each gift and a bell for the star. Keep the bells soft and low-passed so they never sound harsh. Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice bm_george, one line per headline, ending with "Ferrero Rocher. The golden gift." Check every line with Whisper, duck the music and the effects under the voice and master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: first render the opening at half resolution and check it at 10 frames per second. Then export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone marked, and mark where the logo appears. Wait for my approval before the final render.

What came back: one Remotion composition with one three.js world: the snow, the chocolate and its foil, the painted cut, the tree that builds itself, the toy train, Santa’s sleigh and the star, plus the scripts for the music, the sounds and the narration.

00:02 · stills before render · claude code

What came back: the first cut was too perfect, like a diagram, so it was painted again with noise and a real whole hazelnut. The stills also caught the sleigh landing on the train tracks (it now lands in the snow in front of the tree), an ending that pulled back too far and showed too much empty snow, a crowded ending (the sleigh and five gifts moved into the open snow in front) and the headline touching the star. All of it was fixed before the final render.

05 · What Claude built

A real inside and a soft landing.

FOIL

Seven petals

Each petal is its own crumpled mesh with a metallic gold material, hinged at the base and opened one after another.

CUT

Painted pixel by pixel

The cut face is a canvas texture: shell, wafer, cream and a whole hazelnut, with noise on every edge so nothing is a perfect circle.

TREE

65 chocolates

They fall in order onto nine rings that shrink toward the top, each with its own bounce and its own musical note.

SANTA

A spline flight

The sleigh follows a Catmull-Rom curve around the tree and lands with an ease-out in front of it, dropping five gifts in the snow.

const edge = nutRad(th) * (1 + 0.06 * (vnoise(x * 9, y * 9) - 0.5));
const nut = (sn / 0.37) ** 2 + (c / 0.45) ** 2 < 1;              // the whole hazelnut, with its tip
paint(nut ? HAZEL : r < wafIn ? CREAM : r < wafOut ? WAFER : SHELL);

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

Not too perfect

A flawless cut looked like a diagram. A little noise and an irregular hazelnut make it look like a real chocolate.

02

Add, don’t replace

When the train came in, the gifts under the tree came back too. Each new element adds to the scene instead of taking something out.

03

Land where there is room

The sleigh first landed on the tracks and the ending felt crowded. Landing in the open snow in front of the tree gave it space.

04

A bigger brand

A larger label on the hero chocolate and a bigger star made the brand read from the first second to the last.

05

Soft bells

Christmas bells get harsh fast. Lower and low-passed, they sound pretty instead of loud.

06

Stills before render

Rounds of stills, plus the cut and the landing at 10 frames per second, caught every problem in minutes.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Missing textures

A texture that loads after the first frame never shows in the render. Wait for it, call advance() and only then continue the render.

A flipping sleigh

Pointing the sleigh with setFromUnitVectors made it flip upside down on the curve. Use Euler angles with a fixed order.

Text on the star

The headline under the logo touched the star. Move the logo and the line up, and the tree a little down.

Loud endings

Bells, a train and a voice at once get strident. Duck the effects under the narration and low-pass the bells.

Brand names in TTS

Kokoro said “Rocha”. Write “Roshay” in the narration script and check every line with Whisper.

A math quirk

The bundler rejects -x ** 2. Write -(x ** 2).

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Snow, bokeh and gold foil turn to mush at low bitrates. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

The last frame: the logo, the tagline, the lit tree and Santa with the gifts. The feed crops covers to 4:5 and the profile grid to 3:4, so keep everything between y 300 and 1480.

Step 04

A short caption.

What the brand’s style is, that it was made with Claude Code, the keyword, and end with “Inspired by @brand”.

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 7M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -

Before you post: everything inside x 90–960 and y 250–1480 · the logo in the scene at least every 6 seconds · no text on screen for less than 1.5 s · something moves on frame 0 · −14 LUFS · 12–16 Mbps.
