01 · The idea

Every genre, its own technique.

Streaming ads usually scroll through rows of posters. Here the promise is the product: every genre in one place. So each genre becomes its own world with its own motion technique, and the spot jumps to a new one every three seconds, cut on the beat. One giant word per world keeps it readable at phone size.

It is a spec piece built by the Lab. The N is redrawn in code, and every title on screen is a genre, never a real show.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

A spot like this depends on one rule: no two worlds can look alike. Everything else follows from it.

Technique per world

Particles, hyperspace streaks, a shader-drawn eye, domain-warped ink, a flat poster, a lit planet and a log-polar tunnel. Six looks a camera can't film, all in one shader.

Brand rules

The N redrawn in code with its two reds, no real titles or posters, and "Netflix and the N symbol are trademarks of Netflix, Inc." in the caption.

Palette

Black and Netflix red everywhere, with one guest color per world: ice blue, bone white, pink, yellow, ocean blue.

#000000#B1060F#E50914#FF5FA2#FFD400#5FB4FF
Type

Inter Tight 900 for the genre words, Playfair Display italic for romance, JetBrains Mono for the HUD. Each word slams in letter by letter.

Pace

120 BPM, so every cut lands on a beat: 2.5 seconds of opening, six worlds of 3 seconds, 3.5 seconds of lockup. One idea per world.

Format

9:16, 24 s, 30 fps, with every word, block and HUD label inside the Reels safe zone.

Sound

An original score in code that changes instrument with every world, a film-splice click on each cut and an eight-line narration.

03 · One-time setup

Before the prompt.

Step 01

Install Claude Code.

Follow the official guide at docs.claude.com (Claude Code overview) for your system.

Step 02

Install Node 22+ and FFmpeg.

Start a Remotion project with npx create-video@latest, add @remotion/three, three, @react-three/fiber and @remotion/google-fonts, and open Claude Code inside that folder.

Step 03

Python for the sound.

numpy and scipy compose the music and SFX. Kokoro TTS reads the narration; if it won't install on your machine, ask Claude for the lines as separate files and use any TTS.

Step 04

A GPU-friendly render.

Shaders render through Chrome. Pass --gl=angle to every render and still command, or the canvas comes out black.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The whole spot comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: stills before rendering, then the final render.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 24-second vertical spec ad for Netflix called "Every Genre" in Remotion (React), 1080x1920 at 30 fps, 120 BPM (one beat = 15 frames). The idea: Netflix is every genre in one place, so every genre gets its own motion technique and its own giant word, and the spot switches worlds every 3 seconds, cut on the beat. Draw all the backgrounds in ONE full-screen fragment shader with @remotion/three (a uniform picks the scene and its local time), put the type and a film HUD on top in HTML, and render with --gl=angle. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

Brand: the N symbol drawn in code (legs #B1060F, diagonal #E50914 on top), Netflix red #E50914 as the accent, black backgrounds. No real titles or posters.

Scenes, in order:
1. 0-2.5s: on frame 0, 90,000 particles are already swirling (red plus the six genre colors). They gather into the N by 1.6s (GL points whose position is a mix between a swirl and a target point inside the N), hold, and explode outward at 2.0s.
2. 2.5-5.5s SCI-FI: hyperspace. Star streaks shoot out from the center in three layers, accelerating. The word "SCI-FI" in chrome (a silver gradient clipped to the text) with red/cyan chromatic aberration, tilted back in perspective, slowly pushing in.
3. 5.5-8.5s HORROR: an eye drawn by the shader. Almond lids open, the iris has fibers made of noise, the pupil dilates, red veins on the sclera, one blink at 2.2s, a slight flicker. The word "HORROR." under the eye, flickering letter by letter.
4. 8.5-11.5s ROMANCE: red and pink ink made with domain warping (fbm inside fbm inside fbm), always flowing. The word "Romance" in a heavy italic serif with a soft pink glow.
5. 11.5-14.5s ANIME: a flat poster on yellow with black speed lines that flicker, a red block and a blue block that slam in from the sides, a black halftone sun, and "ANIME!" in white with a thick black outline and a hard red shadow, skewed.
6. 14.5-17.5s DOCUMENTARY: a planet at sunrise. A sphere with continents, clouds, city lights on the night side and a blue atmosphere rim, the sun rising on the limb with a horizontal flare, and stars. "DOCUMENTARY" in thin wide type and a counter that rolls up to 8,200,000,000 with "stories on one planet".
7. 17.5-20.5s THRILLER: an endless tunnel of red and white frames (log-polar zoom, twisting with depth) that pulses like a heartbeat. "THRILLER" with glitching letters and RGB split.
8. 20.5-24s: particles in all the genre colors close in from a ring and form the N again; a solid N fades in on top, a double hit at 22.0s punches it and sends out a red ring, then "Every genre. One place." End on the N.

Type: each word slams in letter by letter from the camera (scale 2.2 to 1, blur to sharp) and flies out in the last 5 frames. Words fill the width of the safe zone. HUD on every genre: corner brackets on the safe zone, "GENRE 03/06" with a blinking red dot, a running timecode, the genre name and six progress segments at the bottom. Keep all text inside x 90-960 and y 250-1480.

Audio: an original score made in code (numpy) at 120 BPM in D minor, with a quiet projector tick tying it together and a different instrument per genre (sci-fi saw arpeggio, horror cluster and string screech with heartbeats, romance warm FM keys, anime taiko and square-wave stabs, documentary swell and timpani, thriller clock and a D-Eb ostinato). A different film-splice click on every cut and SFX on real events only (the N forming, the explosion, the warp, the blink, the blocks slamming in, the counter, the final double hit). Narration with Kokoro TTS, voice af_heart: "Tonight, pick a world." / "Go further." / "Don't look away." / "Fall hard." / "Go bigger." / "See everything." / "Trust no one." / "Every genre. One place." Duck the music under the voice and master to -14 LUFS with a true peak at or below -1.5 dB.

Process: before the full render, export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) at half resolution with the safe zone marked. Wait for my approval, apply my notes, and only then render the final MP4, plus a version without sound.

What came back: a Remotion composition with one full-screen shader holding the six worlds, a particle system for the N, a layer of genre words and the film HUD on top, and the music, SFX and narration placed on the timeline.

05 · What Claude built

Six worlds in one shader.

STRUCTURE

One shader, a scene uniform

The shader gets the scene number and its local time. Each world is one function, so a cut is just a new number on the beat.

THE N

90,000 particles

Each point knows a target inside the N. The vertex shader mixes a swirl with that target over time, so the logo forms, explodes and forms again with no simulation.

MUSIC

Composed in code

120 BPM in D minor with a projector tick underneath and a new instrument for every genre, from a saw arpeggio to taiko drums.

VOICE

Kokoro TTS

Eight short lines in the Heart voice. The music ducks under the voice and the mix sits at −14 LUFS with a true peak at −1.6 dB.

key code · glsl
// one shader, six worlds: the scene uniform picks the branch
if (uScene < 1.5)      col = hyperspace(uv);   // star streaks in polar coordinates
else if (uScene < 2.5) col = eye(uv);          // lids, iris fibers, veins, blink
else if (uScene < 3.5) col = ink(uv);          // fbm(q + 4.*fbm(q + 4.*fbm(q)))
else if (uScene < 4.5) col = speedlines(uv);   // anime poster background
else if (uScene < 5.5) col = planet(uv);       // sphere, clouds, atmosphere, sunrise
else                   col = tunnel(uv);       // log-polar frames that never end

// thriller: an endless tunnel of frames
float d = max(abs(p.x) * 1.55, abs(p.y));
float z = log(d) * 2.4 + uL * 2.2;              // zooms forever
float line = smoothstep(0.07, 0.0, abs(fract(z) - 0.5) - 0.03);
LEVEL UP

Swap a world, keep the rhythm

Each world is a separate function. Replace one (caustics, smoke, a city at night) and the cut, the word and the HUD keep working.

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

A technique per world

The first version used one beautiful effect and then fifteen seconds of catalog cards. It looked thin. Rebuilding it as six techniques made it a show.

02

Say the brand on frame 0

The particles are already swirling on the first frame and become the N by 1.6 seconds, so nobody wonders whose ad it is.

03

One giant word per world

Fast doesn't mean crowded. Each world says one word, big enough to read on a phone, for three full seconds.

04

Cut on the beat

At 120 BPM a beat is 15 frames, so every world starts on a beat and the music changes instrument with the picture.

05

Open and close with the N

The particles build the N at the start and, in every genre color, build it again at the end. The spot feels like one loop.

06

Stills before render

A shader render takes minutes; a still takes seconds. Every fix was found on a contact sheet first.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Too many particles, too little light

With additive blending and 2 px points the swarm reads as gray noise. Use 3 px points at full opacity on frame 0, then let them settle as the N forms.

Things off screen

In 9:16 the screen is only 0.56 units wide. A ring of particles that looks fine in a square test ends up off screen: scale it to the vertical frame.

Blend modes change the brand color

A white word in "difference" mode over red ink turns cyan. Check every blend on the stills against the brand colors.

Crashes on long renders

If memory runs low the browser crashes mid-render. Render in chunks of 180 frames with --frames and --concurrency=1, then join them with ffmpeg concat.

The narration can mishear

Run every TTS line through Whisper. "See it all" came back as "See you at all", so the line became "See everything."

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Instagram compresses again, so re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the brand best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 28M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "alimiter=limit=0.8:level=false" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
