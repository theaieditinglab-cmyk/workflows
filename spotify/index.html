01 · The idea

One phone, one journey.

App ads usually show a screen recording. Here the app is an object: a real 3D phone that flies, tilts and steps back, with the interface rebuilt in code on its glass. Things leave the screen and float in the space around it: mixes, genre tiles, a carousel of covers, the song you just saved. The viewer follows one path through the app, so four features read as one story.

The style comes from the clean app tours of @riki.bosso: dark stage, one hero object, floating cards with depth of field and 2-3 word headlines with a period.

02 · References

What Claude needed before the first prompt.

An app tour is only as good as the app you rebuild. Give Claude the screens, the palette and the beats, and the rest is choreography.

Style

Clean premium app tours in the style of @riki.bosso: one hero object on a dark stage, a glow in the brand color, floating cards, short headlines.

The app

Three screens rebuilt in code at 393×852 pt: Home (chips, shortcuts, "Made For You", mini player), Search ("Browse all" genres) and Now Playing (cover, progress, controls, save).

Palette

Pure black stage with a green glow; the app's own dark greys inside the phone.

Type

Inter Tight 700 at 98 px for the headlines, Inter Tight 500 at 34 px in grey for the sublines, Figtree for the app UI.

Beats

Logo, Home, Search, Discover, Save, logo. One action per block of about three seconds, each with its own headline.

Pace

120 BPM, so one beat is 15 frames. The phone lands on the drop at 2.5 s and the big moves sit on the bar lines.

Sound

An original synth-pop track in F at 120 BPM made in Python, plus soft, round app SFX on every tap, pop and whoosh. Narration added later with Kokoro TTS.

04 · Claude session

One prompt, then the checks.

The tour comes from one master prompt. The other messages are quality control: stills before rendering, the final render, and the voice.

00:01 · master prompt · claude code
Build a 21-second vertical app tour inspired by Spotify in Remotion (React), 1080x1920 at 30 fps (630 frames). The style is clean premium motion: one hero object on a dark stage, floating cards with depth of field, short headlines. Every value must be a function of the current frame.

Look. A pure black stage with a soft Spotify-green glow (#1ED760) behind the hero: two radial gradients that breathe slowly (brightness 0.85 to 1.0 on a sine of the frame) and get stronger on the big beats. On top: a vignette and a light film grain (about 7%). Headlines: Inter Tight 700, 98 px, tight tracking, white, 2-3 words ending in a period, centered at y 1300. Each word rises 40 px and goes from 12 px of blur to sharp, one word every 4 frames. Under it a grey subline: Inter Tight 500, 34 px, white at 50%. The app UI uses Figtree, with Spotify's dark palette: #121212 screen, #181818 cards, #B3B3B3 secondary text, green #1ED760 for active states.

The phone. A REAL 3D phone built in code with three.js through @remotion/three (rounded titanium body, glass, buttons), centered. Camera: fov 24, z 74, near 1, far 150, ThreeCanvas with dpr 2, AgX tone mapping (exposure about 1.15). Light it with drei Environment + Lightformers: a top softbox, two tall side strips, a green ring behind the phone (brighter when the song is saved), a green strip from below, and a light bar that sweeps across the glass when the phone arrives and again on Now Playing. Put the app UI ON its screen with a DOM layer that uses the SAME perspective as the 3D camera: perspective = (H/2) / tan(fov/2), and the transform order rotateX -> rotateY -> translateZ(half the phone depth) -> scale. Do not use drei <Html transform>: it does not show up in Remotion renders. Add a dynamic island and a faint diagonal glare over the screen.

The app, rebuilt in code (393x852 pt, scaled to the screen): Home (filter chips, a grid of shortcuts, "Made For You" mixes, a mini player), Search (search bar, "Browse all" genre grid), Now Playing (big cover, title, progress bar, controls, a save button). Album covers are crisp SVG designs (waves and a moon, Bauhaus shapes, halftone dots, concentric rings, diagonal stripes, a typographic cover): no blurry or fluid art, it looks cheap.

Scenes, in order:
1. 0-2.2s: the Spotify logo with a light sheen sweeping across it. Headline "Press play." / "All the music you love, in one place."
2. 2.4-6.5s: the logo leaves and the phone flies up from below, tilted, and settles (frame 72 to 110) showing Home. Three mix cards ("Daily Mix 1", "Chill Mix", "Discover Weekly") float out of the screen, some in front of the phone and some behind it, with parallax, motion blur and depth-of-field blur by depth. Headline "Made for you." / "Mixes built around your taste."
3. 6.5-10s: Home slides away to Search. The phone straightens, a green ripple taps the search bar, the camera zooms on it and "chill" types in letter by letter. Genre tiles (Pop, Chill, Hip-Hop, Indie, Latin, Mood...) pop out around the phone. Headline "Find your sound." / "Any mood, any genre, one search."
4. 10-13.5s: the phone steps back (smaller, faded, blurred) and a 3D cover carousel slides in front: covers rotate +/-40 degrees by distance from the center, get blurrier and darker the further out they are, have motion blur while sliding and a soft reflection below. It steps three times, the center cover is picked (green glow) and flies onto the phone's Now Playing screen. Headline "Something new." / "Fresh finds, picked for you every week."
5. 13.5-17.5s: Now Playing ("Slow Tides", by a made-up artist). The camera zooms on the save button, a green ripple taps it, it turns into a check. A "Liked Songs" card floats in at the top right, the cover flies from the button into it in an arc, and the count goes 248 -> 249 in green. Headline "Yours now." / "Save it. Play it anywhere."
6. 17.5-21s: the phone drops out of frame and the Spotify logo comes back and holds to the end.

Framing: keep every card, tile, the phone and every text inside the Instagram Reels safe zone (x 90-960, y 250-1480). In the wide shots scale the phone to 0.9x and move it up 40 px so it never touches the headline.

Audio. Compose an original synth-pop track in Python (numpy + scipy), 120 BPM in F, progression Fmaj9 - Am7 - Dm9 - G11 in 2-second bars. Pad and noise swell over the logo, the drop with an impact at 2.5s when the phone lands (kick, clap, hats, bass, a pad whose filter opens), a snare roll into Search, a short break and a reverse swell before Discover, a plucked arpeggio with a 3/16 delay, a little bell melody on Now Playing, and a last F chord with a sub at 17.5s that fades out. SFX only on real events, sounding like a soft, round music app: airy whooshes for the phone and cards, soft bubbles as the mixes float out, a tap on the search bar, key clicks for "chill", each genre tile as a plucked note, small detent clicks as the carousel steps, a plip when the cover lands, a three-note musical "like" on save, and ONE chime on the final logo. Export music, SFX and mix as separate WAVs (music + SFX must add up to the mix).

Process. First make a 10-second test at half resolution. Before the full render, export a strip of stills every 1.5 seconds (including frame 0) with the safe zone drawn in red so I can review them. Wait for my approval, apply my notes, and only then render the final MP4 with --gl=angle, plus a second version without sound.

What came back: a Remotion composition of 630 frames: a 3D phone in a ThreeCanvas, a DOM layer with the app on its screen, floating mixes and genre tiles in front of and behind it, a cover carousel, the Liked Songs card, the headlines, and a Python script that writes the music, SFX and mix.

05 · What Claude built

A real phone, a fake app, one camera.

3D

A phone built in code

The phone is a three.js model made of basic shapes, lit by drei Lightformers: softboxes, side strips, a green ring behind it and a light bar that sweeps across the glass.

THE SCREEN

DOM on glass

The app is plain React and CSS. One function returns the phone's position, scale and tilt for each frame, and both the 3D model and the DOM layer read it, so the screen stays glued to the glass.

DEPTH

Blur that means distance

Cards and covers get a Gaussian blur by depth plus a directional motion blur from their speed, so the stage feels deep without any real 3D for them.

MUSIC

Synth pop in F, in code

Fmaj9, Am7, Dm9, G11 at 120 BPM. Music and SFX come out of the same script in three modes (mix, music, SFX) so the stems add up to the mix.

key code · tsx
// the camera and the DOM layer must agree, or the UI slides off the glass
const PERSP = (H / 2) / Math.tan((12 * Math.PI) / 180);   // fov 24 -> tan(12 deg)
<AbsoluteFill style={{perspective: PERSP, perspectiveOrigin: `${W / 2}px ${H / 2}px`}}>
  <div style={{position: 'absolute', left: p.cx - SCR.w / 2, top: p.cy - SCR.h / 2,
    width: SCR.w, height: SCR.h, overflow: 'hidden', background: '#121212',
    transform: `rotateX(${p.rx}deg) rotateY(${p.ry}deg) translateZ(${zpx(p.s)}px) scale(${p.s})`}}>
    <ScreenUI f={f} />   {/* Home, Search, Now Playing: the app rebuilt in code */}
  </div>
</AbsoluteFill>

07 · Decisions

Where the taste is.

01

The app as an object

An earlier flat version wasn't "pro" enough. A real 3D phone that moves through space, with the UI rebuilt in code, reads as a product film instead of a screen recording.

02

Things leave the screen

Mixes, genres, covers and the saved song float out of the phone. That is what makes the stage feel deep and the app feel alive.

03

Short headlines with a period

Two or three words, a full stop and a quiet grey subline. The headline sells the feeling, the subline says the feature.

04

One glow, one color

Black stage, one green glow that breathes and grows on the big beats. The brand color does the work, nothing else competes.

05

Cut to the beat

At 120 BPM a beat is 15 frames. The phone lands on the drop and each scene change sits on a bar line, so the edit feels musical.

06

Sounds from the app's world

Genres sound like notes, the carousel clicks like a dial, the save is a three-note "like". Only one chime, on the final logo.

08 · Watch out

What to check before you render.

Black screen in the render

drei's <Html transform> looks fine in the preview but doesn't show up in Remotion renders. Put the UI in a separate DOM layer with the camera's perspective: (H/2)/tan(fov/2).

UI drifting off the glass

On fast turns the screen slid away from the phone. The CSS transform must follow the 3D rotation in the same order: rotateX, rotateY, translateZ, scale.

Cheap covers

Blurry, fluid album art looked cheap. Crisp SVG designs (waves, Bauhaus shapes, halftone, rings, type) fixed it.

Phone touching the headline

In the wide shots the phone touched the text. Scaling it to 0.9× and moving it up 40 px solved it; the carousel was also made more compact to stay inside the margins.

Render flags

Render 3D with --gl=angle and dpr={2} on the canvas for clean edges. Keep surfaces at least 0.01 apart and the camera at near 1 / far 150 to avoid flicker.

09 · Ship it

From render to post.

The render is not the finish line. These four steps decide how the piece looks once Instagram compresses it.

Step 01

Export at a real bitrate.

Renderers often come out at 5–6 Mbps on flat designs, and Instagram compresses again. Re-encode at a constant 14 Mbps with the command below.

Step 02

Check the loudness.

Aim for −14 LUFS with a true peak at or below −1.5 dB. The AAC encoder can push peaks up, so the command limits the audio before encoding.

Step 03

Make the cover.

Use the frame that shows the product best. The profile grid crops covers to 3:4 (the middle 1080×1440), so keep the headline and the hero between y 240 and 1680.

Step 04

Caption with the disclaimer.

A spec ad says so. End the caption with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

export · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i render.mp4 -c:v libx264 -preset slow -b:v 14M -minrate 14M -maxrate 14M -bufsize 14M -x264-params nal-hrd=cbr -pix_fmt yuv420p -af "aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=0.78:level=false,aresample=48000" -c:a aac -b:a 320k -movflags +faststart final.mp4
check loudness · ffmpeg
ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -
cover + caption · claude code
Export the strongest frame of the final video as a 1080x1920 PNG for the Instagram cover. Show me how it looks cropped to 3:4 (the middle 1080x1440) and make sure the headline and the product sit inside that crop. Then write a caption under 150 words: one hook line, two lines on how it was made, and end with "Spec ad · not commissioned by [brand]. Inspired by [brand]."

Before you post: everything inside x 90–960 and y 250–1480 · no text on screen for less than 1.5 s · something moves on frame 0 · −14 LUFS · 12–16 Mbps · the disclaimer in the caption.
